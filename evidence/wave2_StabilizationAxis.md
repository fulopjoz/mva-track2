# Wave 2 - StabilizationAxis (Q2): chronic stabilization nodes for the N1002K hypomorph allele class

**Ground truth assumed, not re-litigated:** kinase-domain MVA missense alleles are HSP90-client
folders destabilized ~5–10× and cleared by the proteasome (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114);
disease variable = BubR1 ABUNDANCE; steep dose curve (~13 % competent / ~6 % failing);
HSP90 *inhibition* is a genotype-specific CONTRA (the program's mechanism-collision analysis).

---

## VERDICT

**Exactly one feasible chronic stabilization node exists today: pharmacologic HSF1-amplification
with arimoclomol (Miplyffa™) - pediatric-approved (≥ 2 y), oral, 5-year chronic safety record  - 
and even that is a *conditional with-what*, not a candidate:**

1. **No drug in any stabilization class has direct evidence of raising the abundance of a
   destabilized client KINASE.** The supportive chain is all class-level: chaperone capacity is
   limiting for kinase clients (c-RAF; PMID 27703006) and HSF1 restoration raises HSP90 and
   protein-folding capacity in aneuploid human cells (Donnelly 2014; PMID 25205676). Whether
   that lifts a *mutant* client below the ~13 % SAC threshold is unmeasured anywhere - for BubR1
   most of all. **[Gap 1, pivotal]**
2. Arimoclomol's one *clinically proven* mechanism is **HSP70-mediated lysosomal enzyme
   support** (Kirkegaard 2016; PMID 27605553), which solves NPC's storage problem, not a
   cytosolic/nuclear folding-abundance problem. Its human efficacy record outside lysosomal
   disease is two large **failed** phase-2/3–3 programs (ALS, PMID 38782015 - and sIBM,
   unverified here). The label mechanism does NOT translate mechanistically to BubR1.
3. **Every other named class is a NO:** celastrol is a dual-mechanism *client degrader*
   (HSF1 induction + HSP90–Cdc37 disruption + mitochondrial complex-I poisoning) = CONTRA on
   the same logic as geldanamycin, plus cardiotoxicity and zero pediatric data; PBA/TUDCA are
   ER-centric chemical chaperones with good pediatric tolerability (PBA: lifelong pediatric
   standard in urea-cycle disorders) but no plausible route to a cytoplasmic/nuclear HSP90
   client and no such evidence located; proteasome-sparing/degradation-damping has no
   chronically tolerable member - pediatric RCT bortezomib already costs neuropathy and ICU
   admissions on weeks-long cancer courses (PMID 32029509).
4. **A specific CIN hazard shadows chronic HSF1 amplification in a constitutional-CIN child:**
   HSF1 overexpression binds Cdc20, inhibits APC/C, and produces aneuploidy/multinucleation in
   cell culture (PMID 18059335), and Hsf1 loss selectively suppresses lymphomagenesis in
   p53-null mice (PMID 17310987). Graded as a mechanistic flag (overexpression/cell-line and
   mouse data), not a demonstrated drug-level risk - but it makes HSF1 amplification a
   two-edged class in exactly this genotype, worth an explicit safety heading in any submission.

**Bottom line for the attack map:** Stabilization is *not* a monotherapy candidate. It is a
plausible **adjunct layer** - arimoclomol, on-label-class pediatric chronic safety  - 
justified only *after* an N1002K abundance assay shows HSP90/chaperone limitation in the
proband's cells (new assay #5; below), and it cannot substitute for the nonsense-directed
primary axis (Level 1). Celastrol and proteasome modulators are permanently closed; PBA/TUDCA
shelved. The decision analysis's Level-2 sentence "no approved safe chronic stabilizer exists" is
**revised**: a safe chronic stabilizer **does** exist - arimoclomol - but its stabilizing
efficacy *on a kinase client* is unproven ([INFERENCE]-grade rationale only).

## S1 - Arimoclomol (Miplyffa): the only reviewer-class candidate

### Regulatory + pediatric chronic safety (Established - human)
| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| FDA approved 20 Sep 2024 as **Miplyffa (arimoclomol)**, "for use in combination with miglustat for the treatment of neurological manifestations of Niemann-Pick disease type C (NPC) in adult and pediatric patients **2 years of age and older**" | FDA press release 2024-09-20 (fda.gov) + FDA orphan-drug listing (accessdata.fda.gov, approval date 09/20/2024) - both verified this session. **First and only HSF1-amplifier-class drug with a pediatric label.** |
| Pivotal RCT NCT02612129: 50 patients aged **2–18 y**, 12 months; 65 % reduction in annual progression vs placebo; SAEs *fewer* on drug (14.7 % vs 31.3 %); treatment-related SAEs n=2 (urticaria, angioedema) | Mengel/Patterson et al. 2021, *J Inherit Metab Dis* 44:1463. [PMID 34418116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34418116/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jimd.12428) |
| 48-month open-label extension (29 completers): sustained effect, **"well-tolerated over 48 months, no new safety concerns"** → genuine **~5-year chronic exposure**, mostly pediatric | Mengel et al. 2025, *Mol Genet Metab* 145:109189. [PMID 40663813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40663813/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymgme.2025.109189) |

### Mechanism - what it actually does (Established at pathway level; Contested for our purpose)
- Arimoclomol is a hydroxylamine **co-inducer**: it does not activate the heat-shock response
  alone but **amplifies HSF1-mediated chaperone induction in already-stressed cells**
  (mouse SOD1-G93A ALS model: delayed progression, +22 % lifespan - Kieran 2004, *Nat Med*,
  [PMID 15034571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15034571/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm1021)).
- **Its clinically validated effect is lysosomal, not folding-rescue:** recombinant HSP70 and
  arimoclomol restore sphingolipid-enzyme/BMP interactions and reverse lysosomal pathology;
  arimoclomol *recapitulates* rHSP70 (Kirkegaard 2016, *Sci Transl Med*,
  [PMID 27605553](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27605553/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aad9823)).
  Nothing in the NPC body of work demonstrates raised abundance of the mutant NPC1 protein.
- **Client-KINASE evidence: none located for arimoclomol.** The best the class offers:
  - Chaperone capacity is *rate-limiting* for a client kinase: HSP90 overexpression activates
    CRAF and strengthens its signaling pool; folding of the client depends on continuous
    HSP90–Cdc37 service (Mitra 2016, *JBC* 291:24579,
    [PMID 27703006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27703006/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M116.746420)) - *WT client; abundance of a destabilized mutant not shown.*
  - **HSF1 restoration directly raises HSP90 expression and protein-folding capacity in
    aneuploid human cells** (Donnelly 2014, *EMBO J*,
    [PMID 25205676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25205676/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201488648);
    scite tally in repo: s6/c0/m138). This is the *single strongest mechanistic leg* for the
    hypothesis "HSF1 amplification de-limits HSP90 availability for a hard-to-fold client"  - 
    but the substrate monitored was general folding capacity, not a mutant SAC kinase.
  - Yeast/vertebrate precedent in-repo: HSP90 activity is *limiting* for client folding under
    aneuploid proteotoxic stress (Oromendia 2012, v-src readout; repo `LITERATURE_SWEEP.md`).
- **Honesty record (human):** ORARIALS-01 phase-3 ALS trial, 239 adults - **no efficacy**
  (CAFS p=0.62), more GI/discontinuation AEs; sponsor stopped ALS program (Benatar 2024,
  *Lancet Neurol*, [PMID 38782015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38782015/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(24)00134-0)).
  Class inference caveat: NPC success ≠ misfolded-brain-protein success; the failed indication
  is closer to our target biology than the successful one. The phase-2/3 sIBM program also
  failed (company-announced; not independently re-verified this session - see Gap 6).

### The CIN-specific risk flag (Supported as mechanism, INFERENCE for drug-level risk)
- HSF1 binds Cdc20 directly; overexpression inhibits APC/C activity, blocks cyclin B1/securin
  degradation, and **produces aneuploidy and micronuclei** in cell culture (Lee Y-J 2008,
  *Oncogene* 27:2999, [PMID 18059335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18059335/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.onc.1210966)).
  Mitotically, HSF1 is a *bona fide* mitotic regulator (Plk1-phosphorylated, centrosomal)  - 
  Lee 2008 *Cancer Res*, [PMID 18794143](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18794143/).
- Hsf1 **loss** selectively suppresses lymphoma in p53-/- mice (Min 2007, *Oncogene*,
  [PMID 17310987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17310987/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.onc.1210317)) - i.e., HSF1 *supports* tumorigenesis in a checkpoint-defective background.
- Context: these are overexpression/cell-line and knockout-mouse data, not arimoclomol-exposure
  data, and 5 years of pediatric NPC exposure reported no neoplastic signal. But in a child
  whose SAC is quantitatively thin, deliberately amplifying a TF that titrates Cdc20
  (the very node BubR1 restrains) is **the one place in this axis where mechanism and genotype
  collide unfavorably**. Grade: **Contested/flag** - must be disclosed, not a stopper.

### S1 sub-verdict
Feasible chronic pediatric node: **YES**. Demonstrated stabilization of a destabilized client
kinase: **NO evidence located**. Usable only as adjunct, gated by an abundance assay directly
on the proband's allele.

## S2 - HSP70 axis (as a distinct node)
- HSP70 is the downstream effector arimoclomol raises; recombinant HSP70 is CNS-penetrant in
  mice (Kirkegaard 2016). No orally available, pediatric-experienced, *selective* HSP70
  up-modulator exists as an approved drug; BGP-15 (another hydroxylamine HSP co-inducer) has
  preclinical efficacy in mdx/DMD muscle ([PMID 22495301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22495301/)) but **no published pediatric clinical data located** (gap).
- HSP70 inhibition goes the wrong way (destabilizes clients); HSP70 *addition/induction* shares
  every evidence gap of S1 and none of its regulatory status. **Verdict: no independent node.**

## S3 - HSP90/Cdc37 co-chaperone "stabilizers"
- Selective pharmacologic *potentiation* of HSP90 client loading: **no such drug class exists**
  (the attack map's record stands). All Cdc37/HSP90-complex modulators in the literature are
  *disruptors* (see celastrol/withaferin A, below). **Verdict: NO (class absent).**

## S4 - Celastrol - closed, CONTRA
- Celastrol genuinely activates HSF1/HSR transcriptionally (Westerheide 2004, *JBC* 279:56053,
  [PMID 15509580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15509580/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M409267200)) - which is precisely why it *looks* like this axis. But:
- It is simultaneously an **HSP90–Cdc37 complex inhibitor that lowers kinase-client abundance
  in cells** (withaferin A comparator: dose- and time-dependent *decrease of LRRK2*, enhanced by
  celastrol co-treatment; Narayan/Jinwal 2015, [PMID 25989799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25989799/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/1874609808666150520111109)).
  Net direction on a kinase client = the **geldanamycin direction** the attack map already ruled
  CONTRA for this genotype. (Internal conflict of record: Chen 2011, [PMID 21569548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21569548/) found celastrol downregulates HSP90 client proteins via mitochondrial complex-I inhibition/ROS **without** disrupting HSP90–Cdc37
  binding - mechanism disputed, *client-loss direction confirmed by both*.)
- Toxicity: direct mitochondrial complex-I inhibition driving ROS cytotoxicity (PMID 21569548);
  the parent herb's cardiotoxicity is NaV1.5/1.7 channel inhibition (celastrol binds both; the
  largest in-vivo cardiac signal was demethylzeylasteral - Xu 2023, *J Ethnopharmacol*,
  [PMID 37030557](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37030557/) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2023.116448));
  **zero pediatric literature; no approved formulation.**
- **Verdict: CONTRA - permanently closed for this patient, same collision class as HSP90
  ATPase inhibitors, with intrinsic toxicity on top.**

## S5 - Chemical chaperones (PBA, TUDCA)
- **Pediatric chronic tolerability is real - mechanism fit is absent.**
  - Sodium phenylbutyrate is *lifelong* pediatric standard-of-care nitrogen scavenger in
    urea-cycle disorders (Huang 2023 review, [PMID 37807629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37807629/); chronic-use/QoL data Yeowell 2021, [PMID 34694515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34694515/)). So "chronically safe in children" is Established for the drug.
  - BUT PBA/TUDCA act as ER-lumenal chaperones (ER stress relief; e.g., TUDCA ER-stress trial
    Walsh 2016, [PMID 27503949](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27503949/)). BubR1 is a
    cytosolic/nuclear HSP90 client; a targeted literature search found **no evidence that either
    drug raises abundance of a cytosolic/nuclear misfolded protein** (gap-verified, this
    session). No pediatric TUDCA RCT located at all.
- **Verdict: NO node.** These are left on the shelf: good pediatric drugs, wrong compartment,
  no allele-class evidence.

## S6 - Proteasome axis ("proteasome-sparing" logic)
- Mechanistic fact (repo-verified): proteasome inhibition (MG132) rescues mutant-BubR1 abundance
  in vitro (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114 via MECHANISM_G1.md) - i.e., the hypomorph's losses
  run through proteasomal clearance, so damping degradation *would* be the right direction.
- **But no chronically tolerable way to do it exists:**
  - Proteasome inhibitors are acute-cytotoxicity drugs. Even weeks-long oncology-course
    bortezomib in children (AAML1031, N=1097) produced **significantly more peripheral
    neuropathy and ICU admissions** with zero survival benefit (Aplenc 2020, *Haematologica*
    105:1879, [PMID 32029509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32029509) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3324/haematol.2019.220962)).
    Chronic dosing in a non-cancer child is a hard NO.
  - Selective client-level deubiquitination/E3-damping (CHIP/STUB1 arm for HSP90 clients) has
    **no clinical-stage compound** located; USP14 inhibitors (IU1 class) *accelerate*
    proteasomal degradation - exactly the wrong direction for abundance [INFERENCE,
    mechanistic-logic only].
- **Verdict: NO node (in-vitro proof of mechanism only, matching the G1 file).**

## What would change the verdict - gating assay (feeds the join)
**Assay #5 (new for the validation plan):** N1002K vs WT abundance on patient-derived
fibroblasts/lymphoblasts ± arimoclomol (and ± a 17-AAG pulse as positive control for
HSP90-chaperone dependence), read by Western/pulse-chase; secondary readout = nocodazole-arrest
timing (positions the change on the 6–13 % competence curve). This is the exact [INFERENCE] →
data conversion for whether chaperone-limitation is real *for this allele*. If a measurable
abundance rescue appears, arimoclomol becomes a defensible *adjunct-to-ASO/readthrough* layer
under the CIN-hazard disclosure in S1; if not, Level 2 closes permanently as written in the
attack map.

## Gaps (explicit, unresolved)
1. **[Pivotal]** No study anywhere shows an HSF1/HSP amplifier raising a destabilized client
   *kinase's* abundance; N1002K-specific data nonexistent (assay #5 addresses).
2. Arimoclomol's effect on HSP90 (vs HSP70) expression in patients: not directly reported in
   the verified sources - the clinical-mechanism literature centers HSP70/lysosomal support.
3. Whether the HSF1–Cdc20/APC-C CIN hazard (PMID 18059335) occurs at pharmacologic
   arimoclomol exposure: unmeasured; single-lab overexpression data only; no contrasting in-vivo
   human data located.
4. TUDCA pediatric pharmacology/RCTs: none located in PubMed (targeted search, 2026-09-05).
5. PBA/TUDCA rescue of cytosolic/nuclear misfolded clients: no evidence located.
6. Arimoclomol sIBM phase-2/3 failure: known from company reporting; no primary publication
   verified this session (ALS phase-3 failure is verified, PMID 38782015).
