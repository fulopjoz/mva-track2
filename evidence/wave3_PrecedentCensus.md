## 2. Answers to the axis's specific mechanism question (aspirin-Lynch)

**Does aspirin act on CIN / mismatch repair, or on inflammation?** Verified answer: **anti-
inflammatory / antiplatelet, with an MMR-selective-cell-survival effect - not a directed action on
the chromosomal-instability or MMR machinery**:
- CAPP2/CaPP3 benefit does not require repair of the MMR defect; Lynch tumors that arise remain
  MSI-high (no evidence of mutator correction in humans).
- *In vitro*, aspirin (and NO-donating aspirin) selectively kills/impairs MMR-deficient cells and
  suppresses the mutator phenotype (Rüschoff PNAS 1998, PMID 9736731; NO-ASA Cancer Res 2007, PMID
  18006842) - a *selection* against emerging MMR− clones, not repair.
- The leading in-vivo hypothesis is platelet COX-1 inhibition (current review: Pharmacol Rev 2026,
  PMID 41592353); CaPP3's 100-mg non-inferiority (PMID 42425127) is directionally consistent with
  a low-dose antiplatelet mechanism, not a high-dose tissue-COX-2 requirement.
Therefore aspirin-Lynch is an exception to "no prevention drug" **only on the narrow axis of
genomic-instability-by-MMR-deficiency**; it teaches nothing about acting on CIN/BubR1 dose. The
"no CIN-class preventive" claim is untouched by it.

## 3. Scope-safe framing sentence (for the report)

> **Recommended sentence:** "In constitutional *chromosomal*-instability syndromes - the class to
> which mosaic variegated aneuploidy belongs - no pharmacologic cancer-prevention agent is
> approved anywhere in the world and management remains surveillance-only (AACR 2024 workshop,
> Nakano et al. 2024). The two disclosed exceptions that survive scoping belong to other
> mechanism classes: RCT-proven, guideline-recommended aspirin chemoprevention in Lynch syndrome
> (a mismatch-repair-deficiency/MSI disorder whose benefit is anti-inflammatory/antiplatelet and
> whose low-dose sufficiency CaPP3 confirmed in 2026), and approved *mechanism-matched lesion-
> burden control* in non-CIN constitutional tumor-predisposition syndromes (everolimus/sirolimus
> in TSC; selumetinib/mirdametinib in NF1; alpelisib in PROS; belzutifan in VHL; vismodegib for
> BCC burden in Gorlin [investigational, toxicity-limited]). The only drug that ever held a
> prevention-class approval in any constitutional cancer-risk disorder - celecoxib in FAP - was
> withdrawn in 2012 on cardiovascular safety. Proposing a first pharmacologic preventive in MVA
> therefore extends, rather than contradicts, this precedent landscape: the near precedents are
> mechanism-matched pediatric rapalog/MEKi approvals for lesion control, and fully novel
> cancer-prevention pharmacology has one positive proof-of-concept (aspirin-Lynch) and one
> cautionary reversal (celecoxib-FAP)."

**Short form (single sentence, inline):** "No approved pharmacologic cancer-prevention agent
