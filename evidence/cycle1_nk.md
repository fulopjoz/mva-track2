# Evidence brief: Does low-trough (3–7 ng/mL) mTORC1 inhibition suppress or preserve/enhance NK/innate clearance of aneuploid/senescent cells?

Sub-agent: NKSurveillance | Date: 2026-09-12 | Scope: read-only, primary sources, verbatim quotes (PMC full text or PubMed abstract as noted). Superscripts/subscripts were stripped by text extraction in some quotes; noted inline.

**Adjudication question (MVA-1 context, biallelic BUB1B, 5-yo, Wilms/RMS predisposition):** In mTOR-low-dose regimens relevant to conversational dosing (trough 3–7 ng/mL everolimus), does mTORC1 inhibition SUPPRESS or PRESERVE/ENHANCE NK-cell and broader immune clearance of aneuploid/senescent cells?

**Termination criterion (declared up front):** each assigned sub-question covered by ≥1 primary source with verbatim quote, or marked NOT LOCATED. Achieved for all sub-questions.

## Source 1 - Santaguida et al. 2017 (the hard-PENALIZE citation, PMID 28633018)

**Citation:** Santaguida S, Richardson A, Iyer DR, et al., Amon A. "Chromosome Mis-Segregation Generates Cell-Cycle-Arrested Cells with Complex Karyotypes that Are Eliminated by the Immune System." *Developmental Cell* 2017;41(6):638–651.e5. DOI 10.1016/j.devcel.2017.05.022. PMC5536848. **NOTE: journal is Developmental Cell, not Cell as stated in the assignment.**

### Which immune compartment clears aneuploid cells
> "To test whether aneuploid arrested cells were indeed targeted by immune cells we co-cultured euploid cells or aneuploid arrested cells with NK92 cells and followed cell viability by live cell imaging (see STAR methods). NK92 cells did not interact with euploid cells, but effectively killed cells with complex karyotypes [figs]." - Results, PMC5536848

**Critical qualifier - the clearance experiment is IN VITRO (NK92 cell-line co-culture), not in vivo.** The paper's only effector-cell experiment is the NK92 co-culture; no in vivo NK-depletion or clearance experiment is reported. The assignment's premise "NK clearance of newly aneuploid cells in vivo" (attributed to this paper) is a mischaracterization.

### Mechanism of recognition (NKG2D/DNAM1 ligands)
> "MICA and MICB are cell surface proteins that belong to the natural killer group 2, member D (NKG2D) ligand family, and activate NK cells in response to proteotoxic stress. Although euploid cells expressed MICA and MICB at their cell surface, MICA/B levels were elevated two fold in arrested aneuploid cells." - Results

> "Further evidence supporting the conclusion that selective killing occurred stems from the observation that the interaction between NK cells and aneuploid arrested cells was at least partially dependent on the NK cell receptor NKG2D. Pre-incubation of NK cells with blocking antibodies against NKG2D decreased their ability to kill aneuploid arrested cells. We conclude that cells with complex karyotypes elicit an innate immune response aimed at their removal." - Results

> "DNAM1 is an adhesion molecule at the surface of NK cells which mediates interactions between NK cells and target cells. This protein binds to CD112 (also known as Nectin-2) and CD155 (also known as PVR) two surface molecules expressed in response to DNA damage. Aneuploid cells expressed CD112 and CD155 at elevated levels." - Results

### Non-tumor / pre-malignant setting
Experiments used non-transformed hTERT RPE-1 cells (STAR Methods: "RPE-1 hTERT cell lines and MEFs were cultured in DMEM…"). Supporting the relevance of senescence/SASP:
> "Cells with complex karyotypes exhibit features of senescence and produce pro-inflammatory signals that promote their clearance by the immune system." - Summary/Abstract

### Does this paper say anything about mTOR inhibitors?
**NO.** The complete drug list used (STAR Methods, "Drug treatments"): "Reversine was obtained from Cayman Chemical or Sigma-Aldrich and used at a working concentration of 0.5 μM or 2μM; Monastrol (working concentration 100 μM)…; SB203580…; Thymidine…, Aphidicolin…, RO-3306…, Chk2 inhibitor II hydrate…, VE821… and Nocodazole… were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. NMS-P715 (working concentration 1 μM) was obtained from EMD/Millipore." No rapalog, rapamycin, everolimus, or mTOR pathway manipulation anywhere in the paper. **PMID 28633018 therefore cannot support a claim that mTORC1 inhibition blocks NK clearance of aneuploid cells - it only supplies the NK/NKG2D mechanism that such a claim would need to protect.**

## Source 2 - Human constitutional-CIN corroboration: MAD1L1 MVA patient (searched via "MAD1L1 mosaic variegated aneuploidy NK cells" and related)

**Citation:** Villarroya-Beltri C, Osorio A, Torres-Ruiz R, et al. "Biallelic germline mutations in MAD1L1 induce a syndrome of aneuploidy with high tumor susceptibility." *Science Advances* 2022;8(44):eabq5914. DOI 10.1126/sciadv.abq5914. PMID 36322655, PMC9629740. LOCATED (the only MAD1L1+MVA paper in PubMed title/abstract search; earlier "[All Fields]" queries returned 0 hits - a quirk of that search field in this tool).

### Constitutional CIN in a human is accompanied by immune ACTIVATION, including NK/γδ activation signals
> "Single-cell RNA analysis identified mitochondrial stress accompanied by systemic inflammation with enhanced interferon and NFκB signaling both in aneuploid and euploid cells, suggesting a non–cell autonomous response." - Abstract

> "A specific analysis of ligand-receptor interactions [CellPhoneDB] suggested increased signals involved in platelet activation as well as molecules involved in antigen presentation and interferon signaling, natural killer (NK) and γδ T cell activation, recruitment of leukocytes to circulation sites of inflammation, and other general inflammatory signals." - Results ("A systemic inflammation response to aneuploidy")

### Cytotoxic innate compartment expands in vivo in this constitutional-CIN patient
> "Only 30% of the expanded γδ T cell population was aneuploid [fig. S8B], suggesting that the G18 clone arose secondary to an expansion of highly cytotoxic γδ T cells within the immune response against the aneuploidy-associated stress." - Results

> "The expression of some of these molecules is shared with NK cells, which, using in vitro coculture assays, were previously proposed to mediate the immune response against aneuploidy [citing Santaguida-type work]. γδ T cells play a major role in both innate and adaptive immune responses not only against pathogens but also against own tumor and stressed cells. However, their specific links with aneuploidy have not been established yet." - Discussion

> "Exploiting the enhanced immune response associated to aneuploidy could provide new opportunities for the clinical management of these patients." - Discussion

**Interpretation for adjudication:** the one available human constitutional-CIN/MVA case shows an ACTIVATED, cytotoxic innate immune state (interferon/NFκB systemic inflammation, NK/γδ activation signals, clonal expansion of highly cytotoxic γδ T cells), not immune paralysis. This supports the premise that innate cytotoxic surveillance is engaged in constitutional CIN - and provides no evidence that it is a depletable resource that low-dose rapalogs would extinguish.

## Source 3 - Mannick et al. 2014 (assignment's PMID 24927573 is WRONG; correct PMID 25540326)

**Citation:** Mannick JB, Del Giudice G, Lattanzi M, et al. "mTOR inhibition improves immune function in the elderly." *Science Translational Medicine* 2014;6(268):268ra179. DOI 10.1126/scitranslmed.3009892. PMID 25540326. Full text paywalled (no PMC); quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "To begin to assess the effects of mTOR inhibition on human aging-related conditions, we evaluated whether the mTOR inhibitor RAD001 ameliorated immunosenescence (the decline in immune function during aging) in elderly volunteers, as assessed by their response to influenza vaccination. RAD001 enhanced the response to the influenza vaccine by about 20% at doses that were relatively well tolerated." - Abstract

> "RAD001 also reduced the percentage of CD4 and CD8 T lymphocytes expressing the programmed death-1 (PD-1) receptor, which inhibits T cell signaling and is more highly expressed with age. These results raise the possibility that mTOR inhibition may have beneficial effects on immunosenescence in the elderly." - Abstract

**Dose detail:** the specific RAD001 regimens (commonly reported as low-dose daily/weekly schedules) are in the paywalled full text; ClinicalTrials.gov registry lookups did not surface the record with these query terms. Per rules: exact mg doses NOT QUOTED (not verifiable from open primary sources here); abstract-level "low, well-tolerated" characterization stands.

## Source 4 - Mannick et al. 2018 (assignment's PMID 29979249 is WRONG; correct PMID 29997249)

**Citation:** Mannick JB, Morris M, Hockey HP, et al. "TORC1 inhibition enhances immune function and reduces infections in the elderly." *Science Translational Medicine* 2018;10(449):eaaq1564. DOI 10.1126/scitranslmed.aaq1564. PMID 29997249. Full text paywalled (no PMC); quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "The objective of this phase 2a randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial was to determine whether low-dose mTOR inhibitor therapy enhanced immune function and decreased infection rates in 264 elderly subjects given the study drugs for 6 weeks. A low-dose combination of a catalytic (BEZ235) plus an allosteric (RAD001) mTOR inhibitor that selectively inhibits target of rapamycin complex 1 (TORC1) downstream of mTOR was safe and was associated with a significant (P = 0.001) decrease in the rate of infections reported by elderly subjects for a year after study drug initiation." - Abstract

> "In addition, we observed an up-regulation of antiviral gene expression and an improvement in the response to influenza vaccination in this treatment group. Thus, selective TORC1 inhibition has the potential to improve immune function and reduce infections in the elderly." - Abstract

**Interpretation:** selective, LOW-dose mTORC1 inhibition in elderly humans improved real-world infection outcomes and antiviral gene expression - i.e., low-dose TORC1 inhibition ENHANCED clinically meaningful innate/antiviral immune function. This directly contradicts a blanket "mTORC1 inhibition = immunosuppression" premise at conversational doses.

## Source 5 - Direct human data at the exact trough range in question: everolimus 3–7 ng/mL in heart-transplant patients

**Citation:** Díaz-Molina B, Díaz-Bulnes P, Carvajal Palao R, et al. "Early Everolimus Initiation Fails to Counteract the Cytotoxic Response Mediated by CD8+ T and NK Cells in Heart Transplant Patients." *Frontiers in Immunology* 2018;9:2181. DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2018.02181. PMID 30319636, PMC6168668 (open access; full text read).

### Exposure matches the MVA conversational-dosing window
> "Everolimus level; ng/ml - EVL-E 3.38 (1.93–4.75); EVL-L 3.85 (2.7–6.42)" - Table 1 (median, IQ range), with EVL dosed at "1.0–1.5 mg/day" (Materials and Methods, EVL-E group)

### NK compartment at these troughs: expanded, more activated, more cytotoxic
> "We show that early EVL conversion from MMF allows the increase of cytotoxic (CD56CD16+ [superscripts stripped; = CD56dim CD16+]) NK and effector-memory (EM, CD45RACCR7+ [CD45RA−CCR7+]) CD8+ T cell subsets, which show a significantly higher level of expression of cytotoxic molecules, IFN-γ production and degranulation ability under activation." - Abstract

> "The most cytotoxic subset (CD56CD16 [CD56dimCD16+]) was significantly increased (p = 0.002) after early EVL conversion relative to the MMF group." - Results

> "The level of expression on the cell surface of inhibitory KIR3DL1 and NKG2A receptors was significantly lower in NK cells from EVL-E group patients than in those from MMF group patients. No changes were seen in the expression of the analyzed activating receptors." - Results (Table 2 shows median NKG2D expression essentially unchanged: 95.36% MMF vs 93.63% EVL-E)

> "We found that the NK cells from EVL-E patients had significantly higher levels of CD107a expression (p = 0.021) than those from patients treated with MMF." - Results

**Interpretation:** at everolimus troughs of ~3–6 ng/mL in humans - precisely the MVA conversational window - NK cytotoxic cells expanded, shed inhibitory receptors, preserved NKG2D (the receptor Santaguida shows is required for aneuploid-cell clearance), and degranulated MORE than the comparator immunosuppression. This is the single most dose-matched human evidence in this brief.

## Source 6 - Dose-dependence I: oncologic-dose everolimus reduces NK NUMBER (high dose = suppressive)

**Citation:** Besson L, Mery B, Morelle M, et al, Marçais A. "Cutting Edge: mTORC1 Inhibition in Metastatic Breast Cancer Patients Negatively Affects Peripheral NK Cell Maturation and Number." *Journal of Immunology* 2021;206(10):2265–2270. DOI 10.4049/jimmunol.2001215. PMID 33931486. Full text paywalled; quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "We thus performed immunophenotyping of circulating NK cells from metastatic breast cancer patients treated with the mTOR inhibitor everolimus over a three-month period. Everolimus treatment resulted in inhibition of mTORC1 activity in peripheral NK cells, whereas mTORC2 activity was preserved. NK cell homeostasis was profoundly altered with a contraction of the NK cell pool and an overall decrease in their maturation." - Abstract

> "Phenotype and function of the remaining NK cell population was less affected." - Abstract

**Interpretation:** this is the strongest legitimate support for a PENALIZE-type concern - but it is oncologic-dose everolimus in metastatic breast cancer (standard oncology dosing; the paper's dose is in the paywalled full text), i.e., a substantially higher exposure regime than trough 3–7 ng/mL, and even there the residual NK cells' FUNCTION was "less affected." Together with Source 5, this establishes a dose/context gradient: high-dose everolimus contracts the NK pool; low-trough everolimus does not impair (and may augment) NK cytotoxic function.

## Source 7 - Dose-dependence II: everolimus spares NK-mediated lysis in vitro (10 ng/mL)

**Citation:** Demmers MWHJ, Korevaar SS, Betjes MGH, Weimar W, Rowshani AT, Baan CC. "Limited efficacy of immunosuppressive drugs on CD8+ T cell-mediated and natural killer cell-mediated lysis of human renal tubular epithelial cells." *Transplantation* 2014;97(11):1110–8. DOI 10.1097/TP.0000000000000108. PMID 24704664. Quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "Tubular epithelial cell-induced CD8 degranulation and CD8-mediated TEC lysis were preferentially inhibited by tacrolimus and prednisolone, and not by everolimus. Although tacrolimus showed the most inhibitory effect on the degranulation of NK cells, NK cell-mediated TEC lysis was efficiently inhibited by prednisolone (P<0.05)." - Abstract (everolimus tested at 10 ng/mL in vitro)

## Source 8 - Dose-dependence III: low-dose everolimus ENHANCES NK antitumor cytotoxicity in mice

**Citation:** Saparbay J, Tanaka Y, Tanimine N, Ohira M, Ohdan H. "Everolimus enhances TRAIL-mediated anti-tumor activity of liver resident natural killer cells in mice." *Transplant International* 2019;33(2):229–243. DOI 10.1111/tri.13536. PMID 31560810. Quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "In mice that received everolimus (EVR) for 7 days (range: 0.0125-0.25 mg/kg/day), the proportion of splenic NK cells was unchanged, whereas the number of liver NK cells including TRAIL+ NK subpopulation increased for all doses of EVR. Consistently, liver-resident NK cells from the EVR-treated mice displayed enhanced cytotoxicity against TRAIL-sensitive neoplastic cells." - Abstract

> "These findings indicate that EVR treatment enhances the antitumour activity of immature liver-resident NK cells through TRAIL upregulation." - Abstract

## Source 9 - Rapamycin potentiates NKG2D-mediated cytotoxicity of γδ T cells (same receptor axis as aneuploid-cell clearance)

**Citation:** Cao G, Wang Q, Li G, et al. "mTOR inhibition potentiates cytotoxicity of Vγ4 γδ T cells via up-regulating NKG2D and TNF-α." *Journal of Leukocyte Biology* 2016;100(5):1181–1189. DOI 10.1189/jlb.5A0116-053RR. PMID 27256566. Quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "Here, we showed that suppressing mTOR signaling in in vitro-expanded Vγ4 γδ T cells via the mechanistic inhibitor rapamycin enhanced their cytotoxicity against multiple tumor cell lines, and these cells performed better tumor-suppressing effects upon adoptive therapy. Further investigation revealed that elevated cytotoxicity was a result of up-regulation of NKG2D and TNF-α." - Abstract

**Interpretation:** rapamycin UPREGULATED NKG2D on innate cytotoxic lymphocytes - the very receptor Santaguida shows mediates aneuploid-cell clearance (Source 1). Mechanistically, mTOR inhibition can therefore strengthen, not weaken, the NKG2D axis relevant to aneuploid-cell immunosurveillance.

## Source 10 - Counterpoint (PENALIZE-supporting): sirolimus in allogeneic HSCT delays NK reconstitution

**Citation:** Haroun-Izquierdo A, Lanuza PM, Pfefferle A, et al, Malmberg K-J. "Effect of mTOR Inhibition with Sirolimus on Natural Killer Cell Reconstitution in Allogeneic Stem Cell Transplantation." *Transplantation and Cellular Therapy* 2023;29(6):376.e1–376.e11. DOI 10.1016/j.jtct.2023.03.023. PMID 36966873. Quotes from PubMed abstract.

> "Patients who received TAC/SIR as GVHD prophylaxis showed delayed NK cell reconstitution with lower overall NK cell counts and fewer CD56+ and NKG2A+ CD56+ NK cells. Treatment with sirolimus-containing regimens generated similar immune cell profiles as conventional prophylaxis; however, the NK cell compartment seemed to be composed of slightly more mature NK cells. These effects were also present after the completion of GVHD prophylaxis, suggesting that mTOR inhibition with sirolimus leaves a lasting imprint on homeostatic proliferation and NK cell reconstitution following HSCT." - Abstract

**Interpretation (context matters):** lymphodepleted post-transplant reconstitution with combined tacrolimus/sirolimus - not steady-state NK function in an immunocompetent host. Both regimens suppressed proliferative responses; sirolimus's specific incremental effect was lower NK COUNTS with slightly MORE mature phenotype. This is the honest edge of the PENALIZE case, but it does not speak to trough 3–7 ng/mL everolimus in a non-transplant, non-lymphopenic patient.

# Analysis and verdict

**The hard-PENALIZE citation is defective on its own terms.** PMID 28633018 (Santaguida 2017, Dev Cell) contains zero mTOR-inhibitor data; its NK-clearance result is an in vitro NK92 co-culture in non-transformed RPE-1 cells, NKG2D- and DNAM1-ligand-dependent. It establishes that NK/NKG2D-mediated surveillance of aneuploid cells is mechanistically plausible - nothing more. The inference "chronic mTORC1 inhibition blocks NK-cell clearance of aneuploid cells" is an extrapolation with no primary support in that paper.

**What the dose-matched human evidence actually shows (trough ~3–6 ng/mL everolimus):** NK cytotoxic subset expansion, preserved NKG2D, reduced inhibitory receptor expression, increased degranulation (Díaz-Molina, PMID 30319636); everolimus 10 ng/mL in vitro did not preferentially inhibit NK lysis (Demmers, PMID 24704664). Low-dose TORC1 inhibition in elderly humans improved vaccine response and reduced infections (Mannick PMIDs 25540326, 29997249). Preclinical: low-dose everolimus enhanced liver-resident NK antitumor cytotoxicity (Saparbay, PMID 31560810); rapamycin upregulated NKG2D on cytotoxic γδ T cells (Cao, PMID 27256566).

**Where suppression IS documented:** oncologic-dose everolimus in metastatic breast cancer contracted the peripheral NK pool with decreased maturation (Besson, PMID 33931486 - residual NK function "less affected"); sirolimus-containing GVHD prophylaxis in lymphopenic HSCT patients delayed NK reconstitution (Haroun-Izquierdo, PMID 36966873). Both are higher-exposure and/or lymphodepleted contexts, distinct from conversational trough 3–7 ng/mL in an immunocompetent child.

**The human constitutional-CIN anchor (MAD1L1 MVA, PMID 36322655) shows innate immunity ACTIVATED by constitutional aneuploidy** - systemic interferon/NFκB inflammation, NK/γδ activation signals, clonal expansion of highly cytotoxic γδ T cells - i.e., the NK/innate axis the PENALIZE side wants to protect is not a fragile baseline in CIN patients; it is already engaged, and its key activating receptor (NKG2D) is upregulated, not downregulated, by rapamycin in cytotoxic lymphocytes (Cao).

**Evidence gap (state plainly):** no primary study anywhere tests mTOR-inhibitor exposure against NK-mediated clearance of aneuploid or senescent cells specifically. That experiment does not exist in the literature reviewed here. Both sides extrapolate; the question is which direction the dose-matched adjacent evidence points.

**VERDICT (question: at trough 3–7 ng/mL everolimus, does mTORC1 inhibition suppress or preserve/enhance NK/innate clearance of aneuploid/senescent cells?): PRESERVE/enhance - with moderate confidence.** The hard-PENALIZE position rests on a mis-cited paper (no mTOR data; in vitro, not in vivo), while every study performed at or near the 3–7 ng/mL exposure range in immunocompetent humans shows preserved or enhanced NK cytotoxic phenotype/function, and low-dose TORC1 inhibition improved human immune outcomes in two randomized elderly trials. Suppressive NK effects are real but confined to oncologic-dose and post-HSCT contexts. Residual uncertainty: no direct aneuploid-cell clearance endpoint under mTORi in any system - confidence capped at moderate, not high.
