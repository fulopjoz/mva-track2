# T2-06 — BUBR1/BubR1 acetylation: residue-specific evidence record

**Date checked:** 2026-10-19
**Scope:** What is established on BubR1 acetylation at K568 / K668 / K250 (and homologues), by which enzymes, with what effect on BubR1 stability and the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC); whether North et al. (PMC4194088) is correctly described; and the SIRT2-inhibition logic check. Public identifiers only.
**Method:** PubMed search + metadata + full-text reads (PubMed MCP tools; PMC full text). All load-bearing claims were read in the primary text of each paper (full text where noted; publisher-indexed abstract where noted). No correction/errata/retraction notices were located on the two primary papers via [web search on 2026-10-19](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201386907); both remain intact per the live PMC record. ## 1. Executive verdict

| Question (per task) | Answer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Does North et al. 2014 (PMC4194088) report BubR1 **K568** acetylation? | **NO.** The main text names **lysine-668 (K668)** only. K568 appears nowhere in the paper's main text, and PubMed contains **zero** records for "BubR1 K568" or "BubR1 lysine 568" in the whole indexed literature. North's mass spectrometry found *additional* putative acetylated lysines, but the paper states mutating them "did not influence ubiquitination of BubR1 as observed for K668" (Suppl. Fig. S3F); which residues these are is in the supplementary PDF, which was not retrievable in this session (see §4, record N1). | **UNVERIFIABLE — no evidence located** for K568; **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM** that only K668 is asserted in the main text |
| Is North et al. 2014 an **eLife** paper? | **NO.** It is **The EMBO Journal** 2014;33(13):1438–1453. Any "eLife 2014" attribution is wrong. | **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (wrong journal)** |
| K668: direction? | CBP acetylates K668 → promotes ubiquitination → proteasomal degradation. SIRT2 (NAD+ dependent) deacetylates K668 → **stabilizes** BubR1. So **acetylation at K668 is destabilizing**; deacetylation by SIRT2 is stabilizing. | **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (full text)** |
| K250: direction? (hypothesis "K250 acetylation promotes degradation") | **The REVERSE is established.** PCAF acetylates K250 at prometaphase → **inhibits** APC/C–Cdc20-mediated degradation → stabilizes BubR1 and is required for SAC function. K250Q (acetyl-mimetic) is stable and cells never segregate chromosomes; K250R (non-acetylatable) is unstable and mitosis is accelerated. So **acetylation at K250 is stabilizing**, and deacetylation (by SIRT2 per Suematsu 2014, by HDAC2/3 per Park 2017) is destabilizing. | **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (full text; direction opposite to the task's example)** |
| NMN raised BubR1 abundance? System? | **YES — in mice, and in WT MEFs (not in Sirt2−/− MEFs).** Mice: intraperitoneal NMN 500 mg/kg/day × 7 days; BubR1 protein increased in **testes** of both young and aged animals, and in aged animals was restored to 3-month-old levels (Fig. 6D–E). Heart BubR1 in aged mice was "difficult to determine". MEFs: NMN 1 mM, 24 h, induced BubR1 in wild-type MEFs, "almost completely absent" in Sirt2−/− MEFs (Fig. 6F–G), i.e. SIRT2-dependent. | **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (full text)** |
| SIRT2 deacetylation effect on **mitotic arrest**? | North 2014 reports **no mitotic-arrest phenotype** from SIRT2 manipulation — the stability effect is explicitly **cell-cycle independent** (no altered cell-cycle profiles; BubR1 reduced at every cell-cycle stage tested). The mitotic-arrest findings belong to other papers: Inoue 2009 (SIRT2 downregulation **prolongs** chronic mitotic arrest in HCT116), Choi 2009 / Park 2017 (sustained BubR1 acetylation arrests cells in metaphase). | **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM** |
| Logic check: does inhibiting SIRT2 increase or decrease acetylated-BubR1 abundance? | Mechanistically SIRT2 inhibition raises the **acetylated fraction** (measured by Suematsu 2014 at K250). But **total** BubR1 protein **decreases** with sirtuin inhibition (North 2014: nicotinamide, sirtinol, SIRT2 shRNA, Sirt2−/− MEFs), and Suematsu 2014 found the K250 hyperacetylation after SIRT2 knockdown was "not substantial to elevate the levels of total BubR1 or delay the transition from prometaphase to anaphase". So: acetylated **proportion** ↑, **total** protein ↓. A sentence claiming SIRT2 inhibition "increases acetylated-BubR1 abundance" is supportable only for the modified fraction and is contradicted for total abundance. | **CONFIRMED-VERBATIM** |

**Reading the table the right way:** the *acetylation* directions are **site-opposed** — destabilizing at K668, stabilizing at K250 — which is exactly why "acetylation of BubR1 does X" statements without a residue are meaningless, and why a pan-deacetylase (SIRT2 acts at both sites per two independent groups) cannot be assigned a single "direction" by chemistry alone.

## 3. Per-paper evidence records

### N1. North BJ, Rosenberg MA, Jeganathan KB, Hafner AV, Michan S, Dai J, Baker DJ, Cen Y, Wu LE, Sauve AA, van Deursen JM, Rosenzweig A, Sinclair DA. *SIRT2 induces the checkpoint kinase BubR1 to increase lifespan.* **EMBO J** 2014;33(13):1438–1453.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.15252/embj.201386907](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201386907); PMID 24825348; PMC4194088. First author Brian J North; senior/corresponding David A Sinclair. PMC free full text.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (full text read, [PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088/)).
- **Organism/system:** HeLa cells, 293T cells, WT and Sirt2−/− mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs, E14.5); recombinant human SIRT2 produced in bacteria; BubR1H/H hypomorphic mice (Baker 2004 allele, constitutively low BubR1); CAGGS-driven SIRT2 transgenic mice crossed onto BubR1H/H; mouse tissues (heart, testes, spleen, lung).
- **Population sizes:** Lifespan cohort BubR1H/H n=31 vs SIRT2tg/BubR1H/H n=33 (males 16 vs 17; females 15 vs 16). NAD+ hearts: 6-month n=4, 30-month n=4 (males). NMN experiment: n=3 per group (PBS vs NMN; young and aged; heart and testes). Cell experiments: n=3, Student's t-test. Cardiac phenotyping: n=5–14 per group as specified.
- **Interventions/controls:** sirtuin inhibitors nicotinamide and sirtinol vs vehicle; SIRT2 overexpression (retrovirus, FLAG) vs control virus; two SIRT2 shRNAs vs control shRNA; CBP vs GCN5/PCAF/p300 overexpression; MG132, cycloheximide; NMN 500 mg/kg/day IP × 7 days (mice) and 1 mM × 24 h (MEFs); catalytically inactive SIRT2 as activity control.
- **Key quantitative values and locations:**
  - Mass spectrometry detected acetylation on **lysine-668 (K668)** of CBP-acetylated, SIRT2-reversible BubR1 (Fig. 3A; "We detected acetylation on lysine-668 (K668)"); K668 lies in a conserved region **between** the N-terminal APC/C-inhibitory domain and the kinase domain (Fig. 3B).
  - "mutation of additional putative acetylated lysines on BubR1, identified during our mass spectrometry analysis, did not influence ubiquitination of BubR1 as observed for K668" (Suppl. Fig. S3F). **K568 is not named anywhere in the main text**; identity of the other MS lysines could not be checked — the supplementary PDFs were not retrievable in this session (PMC /bin/ link serves an HTML interstitial; alternate path 404). Even if K568 were among them, the paper's own statement is that those lysines did not influence ubiquitination.
  - CBP expression increased BubR1 ubiquitination, reversed by SIRT2 (Fig. 3E); K668Q enhanced ubiquitination and K668R reduced it (Fig. 3F); degradation rate: K668R slower than WT, K668Q faster (Fig. 3G–H, cycloheximide chase).
  - Nicotinamide or sirtinol → reduced BubR1 protein without cell-cycle change (Fig. 1A–C) or mRNA change (Fig. 1D). Sirt2−/− MEFs → reduced BubR1 (Fig. 1E). SIRT2 overexpression in WT MEFs → increased BubR1 (Fig. 1F). SIRT2 shRNA → decreased BubR1 (Fig. 1G,H), at all cell-cycle stages. "Taken together, these results indicate that SIRT2 modulates the abundance of BubR1 in a manner independent of altering the cell cycle."
  - **NMN:** raised NAD+ in heart and testes of young and aged mice (Fig. 6B,C; n=3/group); "BubR1 protein levels were clearly increased in testes following NMN treatment… in both young and aged mice, and more remarkable, BubR1 levels in aged NMN-treated animals were brought back to the levels of the 3-month-old animals" (Fig. 6D–E). In heart tissue of aged animals BubR1 "was difficult to determine". In MEFs, 1 mM NMN 24 h induced BubR1 in WT MEFs and "this induction was almost completely absent in the Sirt2−/− MEFs" (Fig. 6F–G) → SIRT2-dependent; a slight residual increase in Sirt2−/− MEFs was noted by the authors.
  - **Lifespan:** SIRT2tg/BubR1H/H: **+58% median, +21% maximal lifespan** vs BubR1H/H (Fig. 4A; P=0.0384 log-rank); **males +123% median** (P=0.004); **females no change** (P=0.7915). Authors note they were "not able to see a restoration of BubR1 levels… back to wild-type levels" and "many of the age-related phenotypes were not significantly altered".
  - Model (Fig. 7A,B): CBP acetylates BubR1 K668 → ubiquitination → proteasome; SIRT2 deacetylates K668 → stabilization; NAD+ decline with age → SIRT2 activity falls → K668 acetylation by CBP prevails → BubR1 lost.
- **Direct vs indirect relevance to MVA (biallelic BUB1B):** Indirect but relevant. The paper itself links the work to MVA in its introduction ("In humans, mutations in BubR1 have been associated with mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA) syndrome…"). The BubR1H/H mouse is a model of constitutive low BubR1 expression, mechanistically related to hypomorphic-MVA abundance loss. It is NOT a patient-cell or MVA-allele study.
- **Contradictory evidence (internal/adjacent):** none against; limits: (i) no reversal of most progeroid phenotypes; (ii) lifespan benefit sex-limited to males; (iii) heart BubR1 in aged animals not quantifiable; (iv) NMN lifespan was NOT tested (NMN was a 7-day abundance experiment; the lifespan arm used SIRT2 transgenic overexpression).
- **What it does NOT establish:** no BubR1 K568 acetylation; no mitotic-arrest phenotype from SIRT2 manipulation; no patient/MVA-variant data; no evidence that raising NAD+/SIRT2 rescues chromosome segregation or alters tumor risk (aneuploidy was not measured); no evidence that K668 of any MVA-associated missense protein is acetylated/deacetylated the same way; no dose-response of NMN beyond 500 mg/kg/day × 7 days IP.
- **Links:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088/), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24825348/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201386907).

### C1. Choi E, Choe H, Min J, Choi JY, Kim J, Lee H. *BubR1 acetylation at prometaphase is required for modulating APC/C activity and timing of mitosis.* **EMBO J** 2009;28(14):2077–2089.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1038/emboj.2009.123](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2009.123); PMID 19407811; PMC2684026. First author Eunhee Choi; senior author Hyunsook Lee (Seoul National University). PMC free full text.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (full text read, [PMC2684026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2684026/)).
- **Organism/system:** HeLa cells, HeLa–H2B live-cell lines, 293T cells; adenoviral GFP–BubR1 WT/K250R/K250Q constructs (100 MOI); insect-cell-purified recombinant BubR1; endogenous BubR1 assays by anti-Ac-K250-specific antibody.
- **Interventions/controls:** PCAF in vitro acetylation; nocodazole synchronization; TSA (pan-HDAC inhibitor, 100 nM); MG132 (15 μM), cycloheximide (100 μg/ml); siRNA (BubR1, Mad2, APC3, Cdc20, Cdh1); adenovirus rescue of endogenous-BubR1-depleted cells.
- **Key quantitative values and locations:**
  - K250 identified as the acetylation site by deletion mapping + MS; **42-Da shift** on the K250 peptide in M-phase vs interphase (Fig. 3D: 1197.847 → 1239.853). Acetylation occurs exclusively at prometaphase (Fig. 1A, 4B–C); BubR1 interacts with PCAF but not p300 or CBP (Suppl. Fig. 1) at prometaphase kinetochores (Fig. 1B–D).
  - TSA sustained BubR1 levels and delayed mitotic exit **≥5 h** vs controls exiting ~1 h after nocodazole release (Fig. 2A–B).
  - K250R: lower steady-state protein with CHX, restored by MG132; higher ubiquitination than WT; K250Q markedly lower ubiquitination (Fig. 6A–B). K250 acetylation did not affect Cdc20 binding or Plk1-mediated phosphorylation (Fig. 5D–F).
  - Mitotic exit from nocodazole (Table 1): control 0% (n=198); siBubR1 100% (n=128); siMad2 100% (n=97); siBubR1+WT 6% (n=18); **siBubR1+K250R 93% (n=27)**; **siBubR1+K250Q 0% (n=16)**; siMad2+K250Q 94% (n=17).
  - NEBD→anaphase timing (Fig. 7D): control ~53 min (n=60); siBubR1 ~25 min (n=65); WT-rescue ~57 min (n=21); **K250R ~25–40 min (n=43)**; **K250Q: fluorescence did not decline for >3 h; chromosomes never segregated; cells subsequently died** (n=40).
  - BubR1 degradation begins **before** anaphase onset, before Cyclin B degradation (Suppl. Fig. 13); APC/C–Cdc20 degrades BubR1 in mitosis (siCdc20, siAPC3 block it; Cdc20 ubiquitinates BubR1 dose-dependently, Fig. 8A–B); APC/C–Cdh1 degrades the remainder at mitotic exit (Fig. 8C–D). Degrons D-box + 2 KEN boxes adjacent to K250 are required (Fig. 8E).
  - Model: "acetylated BubR1 is a potent inhibitor of the APC/C–Cdc20 complex, whereas unacetylated (or deacetylated) BubR1 readily becomes a substrate of APC/C-dependent proteolysis".
- **Direct vs indirect relevance to MVA:** Indirect. Defines the mechanism by which low-BubR1 states weaken the SAC — K250 acetylation is how prometaphase cells keep BubR1 from being degraded; if BubR1 is already hypomorphic, both a loss of K250 acetylation (faster degradation) and its sustained acetylation (metaphase arrest) are detrimental. No MVA material.
- **Contradictory evidence:** none against the direction; an independent commentary in the same journal issue (Yekezare & Pines) is titled, verbatim, *Escaping the firing squad: **acetylation of BubR1 protects it from degradation** in checkpoint cells* ([10.1038/emboj.2009.149](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2009.149)).
- **What it does NOT establish:** which deacetylase acts (HDAC 1–3 binding noted, not resolved); nothing about K668 or aging; no mouse/organismal data.
- **Caveat for downstream use of K250R:** K250 is also the sumoylation site ([10.1074/jbc.M111.318261](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M111.318261)); K250R phenotypes in any paper may conflate acetylation and sumoylation loss.
- **Links:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2684026/), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19407811/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2009.123).

### C2. Suematsu T, Li Y, Kojima H, Nakajima K, Oshimura M, Inoue T. *Deacetylation of the mitotic checkpoint protein BubR1 at lysine 250 by SIRT2 and subsequent effects on BubR1 degradation during the prometaphase/anaphase transition.* **Biochem Biophys Res Commun** 2014;453(3):588–594.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.09.128](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.09.128); PMID 25285631. No PMC full text.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (PubMed abstract; full text not read in this session).
- **Organism/system:** Human cell lines; specific line not named in the abstract [INFERENCE: HeLa or HCT116, the systems used by this group in refs 9, 15, 16].
- **Key findings (verbatim-capable quotes):** "We showed that SIRT2 deacetylates BubR1 K250 both in vitro and in vivo. We also found that SIRT2 knockdown leads to increased levels of BubR1 acetylation at prometaphase; **however, this increase is not substantial to elevate the levels of total BubR1 or delay the transition from prometaphase to anaphase.** … SIRT2 is a deacetylase for BubR1 K250, although the abnormally prolonged SAC activation observed in SIRT2 knockdown cells is not accompanied by a change in BubR1 levels or by delayed progression from prometaphase to anaphase."
- **Role in the logic check:** This is the paper that measured whether K250 hyperacetylation from SIRT2 knockdown changes total BubR1 — it did **not**. It therefore binds the "acetylated fraction up, total protein not up" conclusion from the SIRT2 side.
- **What it does NOT establish:** full-text detail, cell counts, or blots were not inspected; no MVA relevance.
- **Links:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25285631/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.09.128).

### C3. Park I, Kwon MS, Paik S, Kim H, Lee HO, Choi E, Lee H. *HDAC2/3 binding and deacetylation of BubR1 initiates spindle assembly checkpoint silencing.* **FEBS J** 2017;284(23):4035–4050.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1111/febs.14286](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.14286); PMID 28985013. No PMC.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (PubMed abstract).
- **Key findings:** "Sustained BubR1 acetylation arrests the cells in metaphase, although chromosome congression is achieved." HDAC2 and HDAC3 bind acetylated BubR1 exclusively in mitosis; their deacetylation leads to BubR1 polyubiquitination, degradation, and MCC disassembly — the trigger for SAC silencing. BRCA2 is required for HDAC2/3 association with acetylated BubR1 in nocodazole-arrested cells; BRCA2-dysfunctional cells are resistant to TSA; protecting BubR1 from deacetylation (HDACi) restores/mitoses accordingly. Plk1 is required for BRCA2 (and PCAF) kinetochore localization.
- **Role in the logic check:** provides the mitosis-resident deacetylases (HDAC2/3) that execute the K250 switch; explains why TSA arrests cells in metaphase (Choi 2009) and why BRCA2 loss phenocopies acetylation loss.
- **What it does NOT establish:** the abstract does not name the residue (the group's prior work is K250/K243; the paper does not re-map in the abstract); no MVA relevance.
- **Links:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28985013/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.14286).

### C4. Park I, Lee HO, Choi E, Lee YK, Kwon MS, Min J, Park PG, Lee S, Kong YY, Gong G, Lee H. *Loss of BubR1 acetylation causes defects in spindle assembly checkpoint signaling and promotes tumor formation.* **J Cell Biol** 2013;202(2):295–309.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1083/jcb.201210099](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.201210099); PMID 23878276; PMC3718975.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (PubMed abstract; full text not read).
- **Key findings:** Knock-in mice heterozygous for acetylation-deficient BubR1 (**K243R/+**, mouse homologue of human K250) "spontaneously developed tumors with massive chromosome missegregations". K243R/+ MEFs weakened SAC with shortened mitotic timing; Mad2 kinetochore recruitment intact (signal generation intact) but "because of the premature degradation of K243R-BubR1, the mitotic checkpoint complex disassociated prematurely" (maintenance compromised); BubR1 acetylation also required to counteract excessive Aurora B at kinetochores for stable chromosome–spindle attachment.
- **MVA relevance:** the closest in-vivo mechanistic parallel to a BubR1-insufficiency cancer syndrome: losing the stabilizing acetylation phenocopies BubR1 insufficiency → CIN → tumors. Still not an MVA model.
- **Links:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23878276/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.201210099).

### C5. Park J, Yeu SY, Paik S, Kim H, Choi SY, Lee J, Jang J, Lee S, Koh Y, Lee H. *Loss of BubR1 acetylation provokes replication stress and leads to complex chromosomal rearrangements.* **FEBS J** 2021;288(20):5925–5942.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1111/febs.15912](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.15912); PMID 33955658. No PMC.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (PubMed abstract).
- **Key findings:** K243R/+ mice crossed with p53-deficient mice; genome-wide sequencing + SKY of tumors shows BubR1 acetylation deficiency associated with complex chromosomal rearrangements incl. Robertsonian-like whole-arm translocations; increased collapse of stalled replication forks ("replication stress"), telomere-linked damage. Human cancer mutations disrupting the PCAF–BubR1 acetylation interface were found: **L249F and A251P** (flanking K250) — i.e., the stabilizing-acetylation machinery is a mutational target in human cancer. A subset of human cancer cells with whole-arm translocation also displayed defective BubR1 acetylation.
- **Links:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33955658/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.15912).

### C6. Qiu D, Hou X, Han L, Li X, Ge J, Wang Q. *Sirt2-BubR1 acetylation pathway mediates the effects of advanced maternal age on oocyte quality.* **Aging Cell** 2017;17(1) e12698.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1111/acel.12698](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.12698); PMID 29067790; PMC5770883.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (PubMed abstract).
- **Key findings (note the different sign in meiosis):** In mouse oocytes, Sirt2 protein falls with maternal age; Sirt2 depletion disrupts meiotic maturation, spindle/chromosome organization and kinetochore–microtubule attachments. BubR1-**K243** (= human K250) acetylation-mimetic **K243Q phenocopies Sirt2 knockdown**; non-acetylatable **K243R partly rescues** the Sirt2-depletion deficits; K243R overexpression in aged oocytes suppresses spindle/chromosome anomalies and **lowers aneuploid eggs**.
- **Why this matters to the logic check:** in meiosis the *acetyl-mimetic* is deleterious while the group-preserving (deacetylated) form is protective — the mirror of what stabilizing K250ac does to SAC maintenance in mitosis, because the readout here is oocyte spindle/chromosome integrity rather than SAC duration. Same residue, different functional consequences depending on what's measured. Confirms site-specificity must be paired with assay-specificity.
- **Links:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29067790/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.12698).

### C7. Choi E, Park PG, Lee HO, Lee YK, Kang GH, Lee JW, Han W, Lee HC, Noh DY, Lekomtsev S, Lee H. *BRCA2 fine-tunes the spindle assembly checkpoint through reinforcement of BubR1 acetylation.* **Dev Cell** 2012;22(2):295–308.

- **Identifiers:** DOI [10.1016/j.devcel.2012.01.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2012.01.009); PMID 22340495. No PMC.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (PubMed abstract).

## 4. Logic check (task item c): SIRT2 inhibition and "acetylated-BubR1 abundance"

Question posed: *does inhibiting SIRT2 (a deacetylase) increase or decrease acetylated-BubR1 abundance, given the observed K568/K668/K250 effects?*

The evidence supports **three** distinct truths and **no** single-direction answer:

1. **Acetylated fraction rises.** SIRT2 is a deacetylase at both sites (K668 per North 2014; K250 per Suematsu 2014). Inhibiting it reduces deacetylation, so more BubR1 molecules are acetylated at any instant. Suematsu measured this directly: SIRT2 knockdown increased BubR1 acetylation at prometaphase [10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.09.128](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.09.128).

2. **Total BubR1 protein falls (in proliferating somatic cells).** North 2014: nicotinamide, sirtinol, two independent SIRT2 shRNAs, and Sirt2−/− MEFs **all reduced BubR1 protein**, cell-cycle-independent. Mechanism: at K668, acetylation is destabilizing, and that site is what sets steady-state abundance in this system. Singh 2014 (SIRT1 in melanoma) corroborates the downward direction for a sibling sirtuin. **Therefore "inhibiting SIRT2 increases BubR1" is contradicted by both primary studies.** The only experiment that asks whether K250 hyperacetylation counteracts this (Suematsu) found it did **not** ("not substantial to elevate the levels of total BubR1").

3. **Checkpoint timing and mitotic outcome depend on which site and which phase.** At K250 in mitosis, deacetylase inhibition (TSA; sustained acetylation) **arrests cells in metaphase** (Choi 2009 ≥5 h; Park 2017) — i.e., HDAC-class inhibition prolongs the SAC. SIRT2 downregulation also prolongs chronic mitotic arrest under microtubule inhibitors (Inoue 2009), without changing BubR1 levels (Suematsu 2014). So "deacetylase inhibition → more mitotic arrest" is supported, but via K250/SAC-silencing failure, not via BubR1 abundance.

**What a report sentence may legitimately say (each with its source):**
- "SIRT2 deacetylates BubR1 at K668; acetylation at K668 by CBP promotes ubiquitination and degradation, so SIRT2 activity stabilizes BubR1 — loss of SIRT2 or NAD+ lowers BubR1." (North 2014, full text)
- "At K250, the acetylation direction is opposite: PCAF acetylation protects BubR1 from APC/C-mediated degradation and is required for the spindle checkpoint; deacetylation by SIRT2 and by HDAC2/3 turns the checkpoint off." (Choi 2009; Suematsu 2014; Park 2017)
- "Activating the NAD+/SIRT2 axis (SIRT2 overexpression; NMN 500 mg/kg/day IP × 7 days) raises BubR1 abundance in mice (testes, incl. restoration to young levels in aged animals) and in WT MEFs, and SIRT2 overexpression extended median lifespan 58% in male-biased fashion in the BubR1 hypomorphic mouse." (North 2014, full text)
- "Sirtuin/sirtuin-inhibitor effects on BubR1 in cell lines are consistent in *direction of total protein*: inhibition lowers it." (North 2014; Singh 2014)

**What CANNOT be claimed (flag UNVERIFIABLE in the report text):**
- **BubR1 K568 acetylation** — no evidence located anywhere in the indexed literature; if the report says "K568", it should read **K668**.
- **"North et al., eLife 2014"** — journal is **The EMBO Journal**; if the report attributes it to eLife, that is a citation error.
- **"K250 acetylation promotes degradation"** — the published direction is the reverse (K250 acetylation *inhibits* degradation; K250R is unstable, K250Q stable; K243R/+ mice get tumors from premature BubR1 degradation).
- **"K668 acetylation stabilizes BubR1"** — also reversed (it promotes ubiquitination/degradation; deacetylation stabilizes).
- **"SIRT2 inhibition increases BubR1 abundance"** — contradicted: total protein falls; only the acetylated fraction rises. Inhibiting SIRT2 *cannot* be proposed as a way to raise BubR1, whatever the site logic suggests; conversely *activating* SIRT2/NMN is the direction that raised BubR1 in the only in-vivo test.
- **"North 2014 showed mitotic-arrest effects of SIRT2"** — North is explicitly cell-cycle independent; mitotic-arrest data come from Choi 2009 (TSA/K250Q), Park 2017 (HDAC2/3), and Inoue 2009 (HCT116 microtubule inhibitors).
- **Any direct MVA (biallelic BUB1B) finding** — none of these ten papers used MVA patient cells or an MVA missense allele; all are cell lines, oocytes, or engineered mice.
- Whether an MVA-variant protein's K668 or K250 acetylation is normal, or whether the variant protein responds to SIRT2/NMN — **no evidence located**.
- Whether raising BubR1 by this route rescues chromosome segregation or reduces tumor risk — not measured in North 2014 (aneuploidy was not an endpoint).

**One mechanistic caveat that must travel with any summary:** acetylation at the two validated sites has **opposite** effects on stability (destabilizing at K668, stabilizing at K250), and SIRT2 deacetylates **both**. "SIRT2 → BubR1" has no single sign; the net effect measured only says that in proliferating somatic cells and in mouse tissues, SIRT2 activity correlates with MORE total BubR1, and the site that explains it is K668.
