# T2-04 — EXIST-1 final analysis (Franz 2016) and the Sieben mTORC1 observation

- Date checked: 2026-10-19
- Claim under audit (AUDIT.md, finding T2-04): *"Everolimus prioritization outruns the demonstrated benefit... EXIST-1 supports prolonged everolimus exposure... Its final analysis included 111 exposed patients and a median treatment duration of 47.1 months. This is not evidence for benefit or prevention in BUB1B-associated MVA... Sieben et al. reported mTORC1 hyperactivity associated with muscle pathology in a particular heterozygous BubR1 mouse context."*
- Verdict: **audit claim CONFIRMED.** The EXIST-1 figures verify verbatim; the Sieben mTORC1 finding verifies but its exact allele context is narrow (heterozygous X753 carrier model only) — see Record 2 for the precision required when citing it.
- Convention: CONFIRMED-VERBATIM = value read in the primary text/table at the location given.

## Record 1 — EXIST-1 final analysis (Franz et al., PLoS ONE 2016)

### Bibliographic identity
- Exact title: **"Long-Term Use of Everolimus in Patients with Tuberous Sclerosis Complex: Final Results from the EXIST-1 Study"** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Journal/year: *PLoS ONE* 2016;11(6):e0158476; published 2016-06-28; received 2016-02-03, accepted 2016-06-15.
- First author: **David N. Franz** (Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center; also corresponding author).
- Senior/last author: **Sergiusz Jóźwiak** (Medical University of Warsaw); Novartis employees among authors (N. Berkowitz, J. Niolat); funded by Novartis.
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0158476; PMID 27351628; trial NCT00789828.

### Study organism / system
Human clinical trial: international, prospective, double-blind, placebo-controlled core phase (2:1) of EXIST-1 followed by a preplanned long-term **open-label extension**; this paper is the post-completion final analysis (study concluded 2014-10-02).

### Population size (verified)
- **111 patients received ≥1 dose of everolimus** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Abstract: "Of 111 patients (median age, 9.5 years) who received ≥1 dose of everolimus"; Results → Patients: *"Overall, 111 patients (33 originally assigned to receive placebo) received ≥1 dose of everolimus and were included in this final analysis"*).
- **Median age 9.5 years; range 1.1–27.4 years** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Abstract gives median; Table 1: "Median age (range), years — 9.5 (1.1–27.4)").
- Age categories (Table 1): <3 years 18 (16.2%); 3 to <10 years 41 (36.9%); 10 to <18 years 34 (30.6%); ≥18 years 18 (16.2%) — i.e., **83.8% were <18 years** (Results text) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Sex: 64 (57.7%) male (Table 1). Entry: any age with definitive TSC, ≥1 target SEGA (≥1.0 cm) with serial growth/new lesion/hydrocephalus (Methods).

### Intervention + control
- Everolimus initiated at **4.5 mg/m²/day**, titrated to target blood trough **5–15 ng/mL** (Abstract/Methods) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM. Control in the core phase was placebo (2:1); the long-term analysis is of the combined everolimus-exposed set (including the 33 placebo arm crossovers) in open-label extension; median dose intensity 5.89 mg/m²/day (range 1.0–13.8) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results → Patients).

### Endpoint measures (verified)
- **Median everolimus exposure 47.1 months (range 1.9–58.3)** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Abstract: "(median duration, 47.1 months)"; Results → Patients: *"median duration of everolimus exposure was 47.1 months (range, 1.9–58.3)"*).
- **Primary endpoint — SEGA response** (confirmed ≥50% reduction in sum volume of target SEGA lesions, confirmed on a second scan ≥8 weeks later, absent progression criteria): **57.7% (95% CI 47.9–67.0)**, 64 of 111 — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Abstract; Results → SEGA-Related Results; best overall response improved from 34.6% primary analysis and 48.6% ~2-year interim). Median time to response 5.32 months (95% CI 3.02–5.59). SEGA progression at any time: 13 patients (11.7%); only one discontinued for it.
- **Exploratory — renal angiomyolipoma response** (≥50% reduction in sum volume of target lesions, confirmed): **30 of 41 patients with ≥1 target AML at baseline (73.2%; 95% CI 57.1–85.8)** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Abstract; Results → Renal Angiomyolipoma Response). Median duration of AML response **42.3 months (95% CI 27.8–NA)**; at week 192 (n=19) median AML volume reduction **74.3%** (14.65 → 4.35 cm³). No new AML lesions; no grade ≥2 bleeding.
- **Secondary — skin lesion response** (PGA, complete or ≥50% partial): **61 of 105 (58.1%; 95% CI 48.1–67.7)**, 9 complete, 52 partial — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Abstract; Results).
- **Safety**: 99.1% had ≥1 AE; 89.2% ≥1 treatment-related AE; commonest treatment-related AEs stomatitis 43.2%, mouth ulceration 32.4%; grade 3 treatment-related AEs 36.0% (stomatitis 10.8%, pneumonia 8.1%, neutropenia …); AEs leading to discontinuation **11 (9.9%)** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results → Safety): *"One death (accidental asphyxiation) was reported and was not suspected by the investigator to be treatment related."* Reconciliation note, also CONFIRMED-VERBATIM: the same Safety paragraph states one of those 11 was reclassified — *"the investigator confirmed that the main reason for treatment discontinuation was withdrawal of consent by the subject"* — which is why the Discussion gives *"only **10 patients (9.0%)** discontinuing the study drug because of an AE"* and "nearly three-quarters" completing per protocol.
- **Growth/puberty ascertainment** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Methods → Procedures): *"Sexual maturation was assessed at screening/baseline and annually thereafter using Tanner staging and developmental milestones. For patients aged <18 years at treatment initiation, height and weight were measured at 24-week intervals before and after starting everolimus."* Height/weight summarized as SDS on CDC growth charts (Methods → Statistical Analysis).
- **Growth/puberty results** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results → Growth and Development and Sexual Maturation): >half at Tanner stage 1 at baseline (34/64 male, 27/47 female breast, 25/47 female pubic hair); "normal progression in Tanner stage was usually seen"; median age to Tanner 2: genitalia 12.1 y / pubic hair 12.0 y in males; breast 10.4 y / pubic hair 11.0 y in females; amenorrhea ≥1 episode in 7/31 (22.6%) at-risk females 10–55 y, incl. 3 grade-3 (2 suspected drug-related), no discontinuations; of 93 patients <18 y with evaluable SDS, notably low height 6.5% / weight 14.0% at baseline, notably high height 17.2% / weight 16.1%; *"These percentages did not increase over time after start of everolimus."* Discussion: *"clinical data from this report do not indicate that these areas [growth and sexual maturation] are impacted by use of everolimus."*

### Direct vs indirect relevance to MVA (biallelic BUB1B)
**Indirect.** EXIST-1 is a TSC (hamartoma syndrome driven by mTOR overactivation) therapeutic trial. Its values establish prolonged, heavily pediatric exposure of an mTOR inhibitor (everolimus) with a characterized AE profile and growth/puberty monitoring — i.e., feasibility and safety context for the drug class — but **no efficacy or prevention evidence in BUB1B-associated MVA or any SAC-defect syndrome** (consistent with the audit finding). Note the audit-quoted figures all verify verbatim.

### Contradictory / qualifying evidence (internal to the paper)
- Extension phase open-label; the paper itself states *"the study was not powered or designed to adequately assess other secondary (skin lesions) or exploratory (renal angiomyolipoma) clinical end points"* and *"a majority of patients had not yet completed their sexual development"* — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Limitations).
- SEGA progression still occurred in 11.7%; amenorrhea episodes occurred in 22.6% of at-risk females of reproductive age (2 of 31 suspected drug-related, grade 3).

### What it does NOT establish
- Any benefit — or risk balance — of everolimus in BUB1B loss-of-function / checkpoint-deficiency contexts; cancer-prevention effects; efficacy in children of MVA age groups for the MVA indication; the long-term effects on gonadal function beyond amenorrhea reporting.

## Record 2 — Sieben et al., JCI: the mTORC1 finding and its exact allele context

### Bibliographic identity
- Exact title: **"BubR1 allelic effects drive phenotypic heterogeneity in mosaic-variegated aneuploidy progeria syndrome"** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Journal/year: *Journal of Clinical Investigation* 2020;130(1):171–188 (published online 2019-11-18; print issue 2020-01-02). The assignment's "JCI 2019" refers to the online date; the volume-year is 2020.
- First author: **Cynthia J. Sieben** (Mayo Clinic). Senior/corresponding author: **Jan M. van Deursen** (Mayo Clinic; Darren J. Baker second-to-last).
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1172/JCI126863; PMID 31738183; free full text at jci.org.
- **Editorial notice: Corrigendum**, J Clin Invest. 2020;130(11):6188, DOI 10.1172/JCI144781 — figure-level only: *"the same sample was inadvertently included for the +/L1002P and +/− images in Figure 2E"* plus a Methods wording fix; the online article was updated. It does not touch the mTORC1/sarcopenia claims (Figure 5 / text).

### Study organism / system
Mouse (mixed 129/Sv × C57BL/6) knock-in and classical models mimicking human BUB1B (BUBR1) MVA mutations: **BubR1L1002P** (models human BUBR1 L1012P, the kinase-domain missense allele of an X753/L1012P compound MVA patient), **BubR1X753** (truncated protein), **BubR1H** (hypomorph, low wild-type protein), **BubR1−** (null). Systems: MEFs (aneuploidy, PCS, live-cell imaging of segregation, colcemid and monastrol assays), splenocytes and aged tissues by FISH, tissue RNA-seq (3-month and 8–10-month gastrocnemius and fat; GEO GSE134780/1), single-cell DNA sequencing (PRJEB33810). No human subjects.

### What exactly was reported about mTORC1 hyperactivity — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM
- *Context selection*: *"we conducted genome-wide transcriptome profiling on gastrocnemius muscle from 3-month-old BubR1+/X753, BubR1+/L1002P, and BubR1+/+ mice. This tissue was chosen because in BubR1+/X753 mice it is selectively subject to accelerated aging"* (Results, section "Progeroid BubR1+/X753 mice exhibit hyperactive mTORC1 signaling").
- *Transcriptome*: several hundred DEGs for +/X753 vs +/+; **only 3 DEGs** for +/L1002P vs +/+ (Fig. 5A); STRING enrichment of upregulated DEGs *"were linked to the mTORC1 signaling pathway"* (Fig. 5B).
- *Protein readout*: *"phosphorylation of 2 key mTORC1 substrates, p70 S6 kinase and 4EBP1, were markedly increased in skeletal muscle of 3-month-old BubR1+/X753 mice compared with corresponding lysates from BubR1+/L1002P and BubR1+/+ mice"* (Western blot, **Fig. 5C**; antibodies: pS6K Thr389, p4EBP1 Thr37/46, Methods).
- *Level ruled out as explanation*: *"Skeletal muscle from BubR1+/L1002P and BubR1+/X753 mice had similarly reduced BUBR1 protein levels compared with BubR1+/+ mice, indicating that differences in BUBR1 protein levels are unlikely to account for the differential impact of these MVA mutations on mTORC1 signaling"* (**Fig. 5D**; also 24-month spleen controls, Suppl. Fig. 7).

### In which allele context (the precision required)
- The mTORC1 hyperactivity was observed in **heterozygous (monoallelic) BubR1+/X753 carrier mice only** — a carrier model of the truncated-protein MVA allele. It was **not** seen in +/L1002P (near-normal muscle transcriptome) and not tested/claimed for +/−.
- Muscle phenotype context: per this paper's Discussion and its ref. 21 (Wijshake 2012), *"BubR1+/X753 mice, but not BubR1+/L1002P and BubR1+/− mice, develop multiple progeroid phenotypes observed in BubR1H/H mice, which establish themselves in the second year of life, including sarcopenia, lipodystrophy, and cataract formation"* — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Discussion). The +/X753 sarcopenia (grip strength, fiber atrophy, treadmill work) is characterized in Wijshake et al., PLoS Genet. 2012;8(12):e1003138 (ref. 21 of the paper).
- In the **biallelic** models (BubR1H/L1002P and BubR1H/H), sarcopenia features exist in the paper (less severe in H/L1002P, delayed kyphosis 238 vs 175 days), but **no mTORC1 readout was measured or reported for biallelic muscle** — the paper itself differentiates these models by reduced senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) complexity, not mTORC1.
- Abstract summary: *"mice carrying monoallelic BubR1 mutations were prone to select MVA-related pathologies later in life, with predisposition to sarcopenia correlating with mTORC1 hyperactivity"* — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.

### Whether any BUB1B therapeutic direction is suggested
- **Hypothesis level only, confined to the heterozygous carrier muscle.** Discussion, CONFIRMED-VERBATIM: *"...we found that skeletal muscle from BubR1+/X753 mice is characterized by hyperactive mTORC1 signaling. Chronic hyperactivation of mTORC1 has been linked to skeletal muscle atrophy and induction of cellular senescence (33, 34). This, combined with studies demonstrating that inhibition of mTOR with rapamycin slows age-related deterioration in mice (26), suggests that hyperactive mTORC1 signaling may be a driver of accelerated sarcopenia in BubR1+/X753 mice."* Also: *"BUBR1 and MAD2 have recently been implicated in the control of insulin signaling and metabolic homeostasis, raising the interesting possibility that disruption of this function is linked to uncontrolled mTORC1 activity (20)"* (citing Choi et al., Cell 2016).
- **No mTOR-inhibitor intervention was performed in any BubR1 model in this paper**, and nothing is proposed for biallelic MVA or for BUB1B-directed therapy. The cited rapamycin support (ref. 26: Harrison et al., Nature 2009) is generic aging literature, not a BubR1 experiment.
- Other biallelic-model facts relevant to MVA allele specificity (verified in the same paper): BubR1X753/L1002P embryos fail beyond E3.5 (the human patient with the same combination lived 3.6 months); BubR1H/X753 pups die within 18 hours; viable BubR1H/L1002P mice mimic many MVA features: median lifespan **343 days vs 691 days** wild-type **vs 196 days** H/H (Fig. 6F); *"not prone to spontaneous tumors, with only **13%** of mice dying with macroscopically detectable tumors"* while DMBA lung-tumor incidence/multiplicity were significantly elevated and tumor size increased (unlike H/H) (Fig. 6G); cataract onset **161 vs 168 days** (vs H/H; Fig. 7A); lipodystrophy recapitulated (Fig. 7F/G); kyphosis delayed **238 vs 175 days** (Fig. 7B); cardiac stress intolerance — 50% dead at **18** (H/L1002P) vs **5** (H/H) isoproterenol injections at 4–7 months (Fig. 7H); threshold summary in Discussion: *"A reduction of approximately 40%–90% in BUBR1 protein levels **results in** tumor predisposition or mild premature-aging phenotypes, whereas a reduction of greater than 90% **results in** progeroid phenotypes or early postnatal lethality"* (Suppl. Table 5).

### Direct vs indirect relevance to MVA (biallelic BUB1B)
The paper is **direct** about BUB1B-associated MVA disease biology (it models the patient's mutant alleles and demonstrates allele effects beyond protein level and aneuploidy rate). The **mTORC1 statement itself is indirect** for the biallelic patient context: it comes from a heterozygous carrier of one specific allele (X753), at pre-pathology 3 months, in gastrocnemius, and is explicitly framed as correlational. Any transfer to biallelic BUB1B deficiency (or to everolimus prioritization in a child) is an inference the paper does not make.

### Contradictory / qualifying evidence
- Allele specificity cuts both ways: +/L1002P and +/− did NOT show the progeroid cluster or muscle transcriptome changes, so mTORC1 hyperactivity is **not a general property of heterozygous BUBR1 loss**; X753 is also functionally distinct from the kinase-domain missense alleles that dominate the biallelic MVA population.
- Corrigendum = sample mix-up in Fig. 2E (spontaneous lymphoma mitotic index), corrected; no effect on the mTORC1 claim.
- The paper proposes a mechanistic hypothesis (insulin-signaling control by BUBR1/MAD2 → mTORC1) as "an interesting possibility", i.e., untested speculation.

### What it does NOT establish
- mTORC1 status in biallelic BubR1 deficiency (any tissue, any age).
- That mTORC1 inhibition (rapamycin/everolimus) ameliorates any BubR1 phenotype — no intervention tested.
- Anything about the human heterozygous carrier state (mice only).
- Any checkpoint-restoration or abundance-raising direction.
