# T2-05 — Baker 2013 Nat Cell Biol: sustained BubR1 overexpression, and which paper says what

- Date checked: 2026-10-19
- Claim under audit (AUDIT.md, finding T2-05): *"Baker et al. reported that sustained BubR1 overexpression protected transgenic mice against aneuploidy and tumorigenesis; the paper also describes rescue of phenotypes in a hypomorphic background. This does not establish a deliverable human therapy. It does, however, require revision of claims that this protein class has only been investigated as an oncology knockdown target or that increased BubR1 is necessarily tumor-promoting."* Plus the assignment's sub-questions: exact transgenic fold-increase; whether sustained overexpression protected against aneuploidy/tumorigenesis; which hypomorph-background phenotypes were rescued; and whether the "Nature 2016 aged-mice" paper is the same study.
- Verdict: **audit claim CONFIRMED.** All load-bearing values verify verbatim. Two precision flags are recorded below: (i) there is **no "2-fold overexpression" line** in the paper — the unprotected line T7 is ~5× wild-type (2-fold *below* the protective line); (ii) the Nature 2016 senescent-cell-clearance study is a **different paper**, not the same study.

## Record — Baker et al., Nature Cell Biology 2013

### Bibliographic identity
- Exact title: **"Increased expression of BubR1 protects against aneuploidy and cancer and extends healthy lifespan"** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Journal/year: *Nature Cell Biology* 2013;15(1):96–102; Epub 2012-12-16.
- First author: **Darren J. Baker** (Mayo Clinic, Rochester); equal co-first: Meelad M. Dawlaty. Senior/corresponding author: **Jan M. van Deursen**.
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1038/ncb2643; PMID 23242215; PMCID PMC3707109 (NIHMS author manuscript read here; commentary: Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol 2013;14(10):611, DOI 10.1038/nrm3666).

### Study organism / system
Transgenic mice (mixed C57BL/6–SV129) expressing Flag-tagged murine BubR1 under the ubiquitous CAGGS promoter with IRES-EGFP marker: two independent lines, **T7 ("moderate" expression)** and **T23 ("high" expression)**. Companion systems: WT littermates, T-GFP (CAGGS-EGFP) transgenic controls, primary MEFs (karyotyping, live-cell imaging of segregation, nocodazole/colcemid checkpoint assays), DMBA whole-body carcinogenesis, KrasLA1 oncogenic lung model, retroviral HRasG12V in MEFs, crosses with BubR1H/H hypomorphs, Rae1+/-20 MEFs and Bub1T85 MEFs.

### Population size
Mouse cohorts, mixed gender (n per figure legend: e.g., n=5 males per genotype per age group for healthspan panel; n=3–4 for FISH/karyotype; survival cohorts sized per legend, exact n shown in Fig. 3a/b legends as "mixed gender per genotype" — not individually stated in text).

### Intervention + control
Germline transgenic overexpression of Flag-BubR1 (constitutive, lifelong) vs wild-type littermates and T-GFP transgene controls; in mechanistic assays, T23 background combined with checkpoint-defective MEF lines (low Rae1) or error-correction-defective lines (Bub1T85), and with oncogenic HRas; in the lesion-rescue experiment, transgenes crossed into the **BubR1H/H (~10% residual BubR1) hypomorphic background**.

### Endpoint measures
Aneuploidy (metaphase counts on MEFs/splenocytes; interphase FISH for chromosomes 4 and 7 on tissues, Table 1); segregation fidelity and checkpoint activity (live-cell imaging, challenge assays); tumorigenesis (DMBA skin+lung; KrasLA1 lung tumors; spontaneous tumor-free survival); lifespan (median and 90th-percentile maximum, Wang/Allison); healthspan (gastrocnemius fiber diameter, treadmill performance, glomerulosclerosis, BUN, cardiac stress tolerance/fibrosis, retinal thickness, osteoporosis, cataracts, γ-H2AX foci, BrdU, ROS).

### Verified quantitative values (with locations)
- **Transgenic fold-increase**: Supplementary Fig. S1a legend — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM: *"BubR1 protein levels in T23 MEFs were about **2-fold higher than in T7 MEFs** and **10-fold higher than in wildtype MEFs**."* (measured on mitotic MEF extracts by Western blot). DERIVED arithmetic from the same sentence: T7 ≈ **5×** wild-type. Main text characterizes T7 as "moderate" and T23 as "high" amounts of Flag-BubR1 (Results, first paragraph).
  - **PRECISION FLAG (i):** The study does NOT contain a "~2-fold overexpression" line. Any earlier framing of the form "a 2-fold increase gave no protection" is **not what this paper measures**; the correct comparison is T23 ≈ 10× WT (protected) vs T7 ≈ 5× WT (not protected), a ~2-fold separation between the two transgenic lines.
- **Hypomorph-background rescue** (BubR1H/H × transgene): Main text — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM: *"Expression of Flag-BubR1 **corrected all premature aging phenotypes** of BubR1H/H mice (Fig. 1c, and data not shown), confirming that Flag-BubR1 protein was functional and adequately expressed."* Fig. 1c caption: *"Both Flag-BubR1 transgenes correct the **growth retardation and aging-associated phenotypes** of BubR1H/H mice."* (i.e., rescue demonstrated on the progeroid/growth panel; phenotypes itemized for the wild-type-background analyses below rather than re-tabulated for H/H).
- **Tumorigenesis protection**: DMBA: *"Only **33% of T23 mice** developed tumors in contrast to **100% of wildtype** animals"* (reductions in both skin and lung tumor incidence; Fig. 2a); T7 "not significantly reduced". KrasLA1: *"At 6 weeks, KrasLA1 mice on average had **17 lung tumors**, whereas T23;KrasLA1 mice only had **8**"* (Fig. 2b); T7;KrasLA1 failed to reduce tumor formation (Suppl. Fig. S2a). Mechanism: T23 reduced Ras-induced aneuploidy (Fig. 2d) and missegregation (Fig. 2e) in MEFs and reduced KrasLA1 lung aneuploidy (Fig. 2f); restored normal checkpoint activity in low-Rae1 MEFs (Fig. 2g) and improved error correction in Bub1T85 MEFs (Fig. 2h) — all CONFIRMED-VERBATIM from Results.
- **Spontaneous tumors and lifespan**: *"development of lethal tumors (i.e. malignant lymphomas, sarcomas, and carcinomas) was **significantly delayed** in T23"* (Fig. 3a); spontaneous tumors arising in T23 animals had **low FLAG-BubR1**, implying selection for loss of transgene expression (Suppl. Fig. S3a/b — important mechanistic caveat); *"T23 mice showed increased longevity"* (Fig. 3b) with tumor protection and lifespan extension in both genders, "more profoundly in males"; **maximum lifespan** also extended vs wild-type (p = 0.05) and vs T-GFP (p = 0.0056) by one-sided Wang/Allison test (Fig. 3b legend) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM. **Exact median-lifespan values: shown on the curves only, not stated numerically in the text — UNVERIFIABLE as a numeric from the text** (the figure gives them graphically).
- **Healthspan (T23 vs wild-type)**: gastrocnemius fiber diameter declines **35%** between 3 and 24 months in WT but is protected in T23 (Fig. 4a); aged T23 mice outperform WT in treadmill time/distance/work (Fig. 4d–f); glomerulosclerosis, interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy reduced (Fig. 4g/h); BUN **16.10 ± 1.95 vs 23.02 ± 2.08 mg/dl** (T23 vs WT, P = 0.0136, unpaired t) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM in text; γ-H2AX foci in kidney: fewer in aged T23, more in young progeroid BubR1H/H (Fig. 4i); cardiac stress tolerance: WT cardiac arrest within **6.5 min** of lethal isoproterenol; 5-month BubR1H/H die ≈3× faster; T23 extended, and WT decline between 5 and 15 months not seen in T23 (Fig. 4j); cardiac interstitial fibrosis reduced (Fig. 4k); retinal atrophy resistance (Suppl. Fig. S4f). **NOT improved**: osteoporosis and cataracts (Suppl. Fig. S4g–i) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM in text ("...two other age-related disorders ... were not delayed").
- **Age-related aneuploidy attenuation (Table 1, interphase FISH, chr 4 / chr 7 at 3 vs 24 months)**: lung 3.7/4.0% (WT, ↑ with age) vs 1.3/1.3% (T23, no ↑); skeletal muscle 13.0/14.0% vs 7.3/8.0%; kidney 5.7/7.3% vs 2.7/2.3%; heart 11.3/11.3% vs 5.3/6.0%; eye 9.3/8.0% vs 4.7/4.7%. NOT attenuated: bone marrow and small intestine (Table 1). Stem-cell compartments (satellite, cardiac, hematopoietic) low in both — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM from Table 1.
- **Threshold**: T7 (moderate) animals were not protected from tumors (Fig. 2a, S2a), age-related aneuploidization or age-related tissue dysfunction (Suppl. Fig. S5, whose title is "Age-related attenuation of tissue dysfunction **requires a threshold level of BubR1 overexpression**"; "Age-related retinal degeneration is not attenuated in T7 mice"; "Cardiac stress tolerance is not improved in T7 animals").
- **No harm from high level**: T7/T23 splenocytes and MEFs showed normal aneuploidy and missegregation rates; checkpoint not hyperactivated at supranormal BubR1 (Fig. 1d–f) — in contrast with overexpressed Bub1, Mad2, UbcH10, which the paper notes cause aneuploidy and tumors.

### Direct vs indirect relevance to MVA (biallelic BUB1B)
**Indirect-to-proximal.** This is the affected gene's own mouse-allele family: it shows that (a) raising BubR1 *above* normal level is protective against induced and spontaneous aneuploidization/tumorigenesis and several age-related degenerations, and (b) restoring BubR1 via transgene **corrects all premature-aging phenotypes of BubR1H/H mice** — the strongest in-vivo support for the "abundance restoration rescues" concept. It is indirect in that the MVA disease lesion is *reduced* abundance, the protection shown is *supranormal* expression from germline transgenesis, and no MVA patient allele is modeled (the directly MVA-relevant alleles are in Sieben JCI 2019/2020, T2-04 Record 2).

### Contradictory / qualifying evidence
- Overexpressing *other* checkpoint factors (Bub1, Mad2, UbcH10) **causes** aneuploidy and tumors; the non-aneuploidogenic outcome with BubR1 (Fig. 1d–f) is a property of this regulator, not of the class — warns against "checkpoint overexpression is safe" generalization.
- T23 tumors that arose had lost high transgene expression (S3a/b) — the protection is expression-dependent, and the trans of a small-mimic remains unproven.
- BubR1(N) caveat from the same group (Weaver et al., eLife 2016, 10.7554/eLife.16620; listed as ref. 15 in Sieben JCI): BubR1 N-terminus "necessary, but not sufficient"; protection tracked the CDC20-binding domain rather than level per se — a qualification of "level is the corrected variable." (Context citation, not this paper's data.)
- Threshold: protection required the high (≈10×) line; the moderate (≈5×) line gave no protection — so "raising BubR1" is not dose-agnostic.

### What it does NOT establish
- Any deliverable human modality (a germline CAGGS transgene is not a therapy; the paper itself notes no apparent side effects **of transgenesis**, not of a drug).
- Efficacy of raising BubR1 in an MVA patient, in any specific patient allele context, or after birth (expression was constitutive from development).
- A validated quantitative therapeutic threshold (e.g., "10-fold needed") transferable to humans — the comparison is two transgenic lines in one colony/background.
- That BubR1 raising is anti-aneuploid in every tissue (bone marrow and small intestine were not attenuated; stem cells showed no age-related rise in either genotype).

## Disambiguation — "that paper also Nature 2016 aged-mice?": NO. Three separate papers

- **Baker et al., Nat Cell Biol 2013** (this record; DOI 10.1038/ncb2643; PMID 23242215) = the **BubR1-overexpression** study. It contains NO senescent-cell-clearance experiment and NO "aged wild-type mice clearance" result.
- **Baker et al., Nature 2016;530(7589):184–189** ("Naturally occurring p16(Ink4a)-positive cells shorten healthy lifespan"; DOI 10.1038/nature16932; PMID 26840489; PMCID PMC4845101) = the **INK-ATTAC p16-senescent-cell clearance in naturally aged mice** study. Abstract verification (2026-10-19): INK-ATTAC transgene + AP20187 twice weekly *"starting at one year of age"* in **wild-type mice**; AP20187 *"extended median lifespan in both male and female mice of two distinct genetic backgrounds"*; clearance *"delayed tumorigenesis and attenuated age-related deterioration of several organs ... including kidney, heart and fat"*. **No BubR1 manipulation anywhere in that paper.**
- For completeness, a third related paper: **Baker et al., Nature 2011;479:232–236** ("Clearance of p16Ink4a-positive senescent cells delays ageing-associated disorders"; DOI 10.1038/nature10600; PMID 22048312) = the INK-ATTAC clearance run in the **BubR1H/H progeroid background** (verified at citation level via the reference lists of both Sieben JCI 2019 and Baker NCB 2013, which carry the identical title/volume/authors/DOI).
- Therefore: if a document attributes "protection of aged mice from age-related pathologies via p16-cell clearance" to the 2013 NCB paper, that conflates two studies; the 2013 paper's aging claims rest on BubR1 overabundance, the 2016 paper's on senescent-cell clearance in unmanipulated BubR1 backgrounds.
