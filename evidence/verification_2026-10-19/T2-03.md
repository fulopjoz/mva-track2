# T2-03 — Branaplam: VIBRANT-HD adult-HD neuropathy, pediatric SMA program, NCT05111249

- Date checked: 2026-10-19
- Claim under audit (AUDIT.md, finding T2-03): *"Branaplam toxicity is assigned to the wrong population. The cited 85.7% peripheral-neuropathy signal was observed in 18 of 21 branaplam-treated participants in VIBRANT-HD, an adult Huntington's disease trial. It is not a pediatric adverse-event rate. The trial record confirms the adult HD setting and termination for suspected neuropathy."*
- Verdict: **audit claim CONFIRMED at primary-source level.** Every load-bearing number below is CONFIRMED-VERBATIM with its source location, except where marked UNVERIFIABLE / DERIVED.
- Convention: CONFIRMED-VERBATIM = value read in the primary text/record at the location given; UNVERIFIABLE = could not be read in the primary text, reason stated.

## Record 1 — VIBRANT-HD primary publication

### Bibliographic identity
- Exact title: **"Oral splicing modulator branaplam in Huntington's disease: a phase 2 randomized controlled trial"** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (article title).
- Journal/year: *Nature Medicine* **2026**;32(1):103–112; Epub 2026 Jan 5. **Correction to the assignment's citation year: the paper is "Nat Med 2026", not 2025** (DOI minted 2025; online publication 2026-01-05; issue January 2026).
- First author: **Beth Borowsky** (Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp.; also the corresponding author, NH, USA).
- Senior/last author: **Blair R. Leavitt** (Centre for Molecular Medicine and Therapeutics, UBC, Vancouver).
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-04117-4; PMID 41491108; PMCID PMC12823413 (full text read there).

### Study organism / system
Adult humans with early-manifest Huntington's disease (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2b), plus preclinical rhesus/cynomolgus nonhuman primate (NHP) toxicology reported in the same paper; prior dog toxicology cited (Theil 2023, ref. 20).

### Population size (trial)
- Planned **75** participants (3 cohorts × ~25, staggered dose-range-finding design); Actual: 61 screened, **26 randomized to cohort 1** (the only cohort ever enrolled) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results, "Study population and early termination": "Overall, only 61 participants were screened, of whom 26 completed screening ... and were randomized to cohort 1"; Methods: n=75 for three arms powered by MCP-Mod).
- Allocation: **21 participants branaplam 56 mg once weekly; 5 placebo** (4:1) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM ("A total of 21 participants received branaplam 56 mg weekly, including 15 who remained in the study until week 69 and 6 who terminated early. Five participants received placebo; they were all part of the study until week 33.").
- Treatment exposure 5–22 weeks; branaplam arm followed up to 1 year (week 69); placebo arm to week 33 — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Eligibility (Methods, Participants): Stage 1 or 2 HD (Shoulson & Fahn), ≥40 CAG repeats, UHDRS-TFC >8; "male and female participants aged **>25 years**"; "An age cap of **75 years** was used". Registry eligibility: 25–75 years inclusive, all sexes.
- Baseline mean age: 50.2 years (SD 10.96) overall; 49.6 (SD 10.06) branaplam arm; 52.8 (SD 15.30) placebo — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (ClinicalTrials.gov baseline characteristics module).
- First participant enrolled 2022-01-10, last 2022-07-04; 12 sites in 5 countries (Canada, France, Germany, Spain, Hungary) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results + registry recruitment details).

### Intervention + control
Branaplam 56 mg oral solution once weekly vs matching placebo (cohort 1 only; planned higher-dose cohorts 112 mg, and 154/84/28 mg, never enrolled). Sponsor: Novartis.

### Endpoint measures
Primary efficacy: % change from baseline in CSF mutant huntingtin (mHTT) at week 17. Primary safety: AEs/SAEs, plus targeted neurotoxicity monitoring (serum NfL with preset cutoffs >100 pg/mL or >2× baseline; nerve conduction studies (NCS: sural SNAP, tibial CMAP); neurological examination; neuropathy questionnaire; lateral-ventricle MRI). Secondary: UHDRS TFC/TMS/IS/cUHDRS/FAS, volumetric MRI, cognition, PK.

### Verified quantitative values (with locations)
- **Peripheral neuropathy signal: "18 (85.7%)"** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM, **Abstract**: *"Of the 21 participants in the initial cohort receiving branaplam 56 mg weekly, 18 (85.7%) showed at least one sign or symptom of peripheral neuropathy."* and **Results → Neuropathy**: *"18 of 21 (85.7%) participants who received branaplam 56 mg weekly had at least one sign or symptom potentially indicative of peripheral neuropathy, as demonstrated by nerve conduction studies (NCS), neurological examination or reported in the neuropathy questionnaire or as an adverse event (AE) (Fig. 4b and Supplementary Table 4)."* Interim-review paragraph: *"85.7% ... had one or more signs or symptoms potentially indicative of peripheral neuropathy ... and 71.4% showed an increase in NfL levels after 9 weeks of treatment."*
- NCS components: **11 of 21 (52.4%)** had ≥50% reduction in sural SNAP amplitude; **3 of 21 (14.3%)** in tibial CMAP amplitude; vs 1 of 5 (20%) placebo with ≥50% reduction in one measure — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results → Neuropathy; Supplementary Table 5A). Sural SNAP returned to baseline at final visit in 4 (36.4%); all 3 tibial cases.
- Neurological examination new findings (weakness / reduced-absent reflexes / sensory loss at two consecutive visits): **13 (61.9%)** branaplam arm; 8 recovered partially, 2 fully at 1-year follow-up; 0/5 placebo — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (same section; Suppl Tables 4, 5B).
- Neuropathy questionnaire-confirmed symptoms: **6 (28.6%)** branaplam vs 1 (20.0%) placebo — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Suppl Tables 4, 5C).
- Neuropathy reported as AE: **13 (61.9%)** branaplam arm vs 1 (paresthesia) placebo; 3 (23.1%) fully recovered — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- NfL: serum NfL increase **76.2%**, CSF NfL increase **61.9%** of branaplam-arm participants vs 0 placebo (preset cutoffs; Fig. 4a, Suppl Table 3) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM; all participants with follow-up data showed reversal of serum NfL increase; findings "either resolved or downgraded in severity at the end of the 1-year follow-up".
- AE burden: AEs in **85.7% (18/21)** branaplam arm vs 40.0% (2/5) placebo; treatment-related 66.7% vs 20.0%; SAEs 19.0% (4/21), all branaplam arm, 14.3% (3/21) treatment-related (subdural hematoma ×2, vestibular neuronitis ×1); no fatal SAEs — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Results → Adverse events; Suppl Table 8).
- PD: CSF mHTT −22.3% (s.e. 3.34; n=17) at week 9 vs −6.4% (15.05; n=5) placebo; −26.6% (7.45; n=9) at week 17 vs −1.4% (10.26; n=4) placebo; **placebo-corrected −25.2% at week 17** (Fig. 6; Discussion: "lowered the levels of mHTT in CSF (by 25% compared with placebo at week 17)") — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Lateral ventricle volume increased on branaplam vs placebo at week 17, transiently, with recovery by week 33 and subsequent rate similar to TRACK-HD external control (Fig. 5a; Discussion).

### Why terminated (verbatim evidence)
- Abstract: *"This safety signal, along with dose-modeling results triggered the early termination of VIBRANT-HD."*
- Results: *"Four months later, following careful monitoring of the study participants and a review of unblinded data by the VIBRANT-HD cohort gating assessment team, it was determined that the benefit–risk ratio was not favorable. The decision was then made to permanently discontinue the study treatment and halt the development of branaplam as a potential treatment for people with HD. This decision was endorsed by the independent Data Monitoring Committee as well as the VIBRANT-HD Steering Committee."* Temporary suspension date: **5 August 2022** ("the study treatment was temporarily suspended on 5 August 2022 because of safety concerns related to signs and signals of potential peripheral neuropathy").
- Modeling: no lower or less-frequent regimen would reach ≥30% CSF-mHTT lowering at lower exposure than 56 mg weekly; "These conclusions were key arguments in the decision to terminate the development of branaplam in HD." — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- Registry `whyStopped` (NCT05111249, verified 2026-10-19): *"The trial was halted prematurely due to signs and symptoms that suggested the possibility of peripheral neuropathy. All participants who received branaplam continued to undergo routine (safety) evaluations for up to a year following their final dose."* Registry also: urgent safety measure (USM) follow-up notification dated **06-Dec-2022** permanently discontinued study treatment.

### Preclinical neuropathy corroboration inside the same paper
NHP 6-week study (n=12 male cynomolgus; 0/0.6/3.0/6.0 mg/kg twice weekly): dose-related serum/CSF NfL increase, no histopathology. NHP 39-week study (n=40): NfL rise from day 29–120 at ≥3 mg/kg; histopathological dying-back axonopathy/dorsal-root-ganglion and peripheral sensory nerve findings at ≥0.6–3 mg/kg; partial-to-full resolution after dosing cessation. Dog: axonal degeneration of peripheral nerves and spinal cord (Theil 2023; ref. 20).

### Direct vs indirect relevance to MVA (biallelic BUB1B)
**Indirect.** Branaplam is an oral SMN2/HTT mRNA splicing modulator; the disease (HD), the pathway and the toxicity mechanism are unrelated to BUB1B biology. Its audit-relevant role is the population assignment: the 85.7% neuropathy figure belongs to an **adult early-manifest HD cohort (mean age ≈50, ages 25–75)**, so it must not be quoted as pediatric evidence. Independently, it is a precedent that an oral small-molecule splicing modulator can lower a disease-associated protein in human CSF (≈25% placebo-corrected CSF mHTT), which is modality context only.

### Contradictory / qualifying evidence
- In the pediatric SMA trial, per this paper's own text: *"careful monitoring of young children who received branaplam for up to 7 years in the SMA trial did not reveal any risk of axonal degeneration"[13] (S. Jevtic et al., manuscript in preparation)* — qualify: the long-term pediatric safety dataset is explicitly **"manuscript in preparation"**, i.e., not yet a citable complete primary source; it rests on ref. 13 (Keller 2022) + unpublished data.
- Mechanism: paper states neuropathy-related signals are *"unlikely to be due to the lowering of HTT levels because branaplam does not modulate HTT mRNA splicing in dogs or NHPs"*; off-target splicing effects invoked; a p53/"nuclear stress" hypothesis (refs. 28–29) is **not supported** by in-vivo dog expression data or by Novartis in-vitro/NHP results "due to be published separately".
- Independent corroboration of the neuropathy: Krach et al., *iScience* 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2025.112380 ("RNA splicing modulator for Huntington's disease treatment induces peripheral neuropathy"), cited in the paper.

### What this record does NOT establish
- Any pediatric safety or efficacy conclusion for branaplam (the trial was adult-only, n=21 drug-exposed, early-terminated, descriptive analysis).
- Any conclusion in a BUB1B/MVA context.
- Dose- or duration-dependence of human neuropathy beyond the single 56 mg weekly regimen (no other human dose cohorts were enrolled).
- Complete reversibility in all participants (reversibility was partial or incomplete by week 69 in several measures: sural SNAP returned to baseline in only 4 (36.4%) of the 11 affected; of the 13 with neurological-exam findings, 8 recovered partially and 2 fully — see the section above for the destinations of each count).

## Record 3 — Pediatric SMA branaplam program status (registry + paper)

- The VIBRANT-HD paper explicitly links its pediatric rationale to **NCT02268552**: *"oral branaplam was also shown to lower blood HTT mRNA expression in children with type I SMA (NCT02268552)"* — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (Introduction).
- **NCT02268552**: brief title "An Open Label Study of LMI070 (Branaplam) in Type 1 Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)"; official title "An Open Label Multi-part First-in-human Study of Oral LMI070 in Infants With Type 1 Spinal Muscular Atrophy"; sponsor Novartis; **Phase 1/Phase 2**, open-label, single-group, multi-part (Part 1: once-weekly dose-escalation, 13 weeks + extension; Part 2: 2-dose cohorts, 52 weeks; Part 3: long-term continuation) — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- **Ages: infants 28 days to 182 days** (eligibility min/max): Part 1 age at screening 1–7 months; Part 2 30–180 days — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM. Population: Type 1 SMA, symptom onset <6 months, genetically confirmed biallelic SMN1 mutation, SMN2 copy number = 2.
- **Enrollment: 40, ACTUAL** — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM.
- **Status: COMPLETED** (`overallStatus = COMPLETED`, verified 2025-03): start 2015-04-02; primary completion and completion 2022-12-29; results first posted 2025-04-09 — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM. Registry records **no termination**.
- Primary endpoints: DLTs in Part 1 (14-day DLT window); TEAEs/SAEs/deaths (baseline to ~83 months). Secondary: PK (AUC/Cmax), growth parameters (length, head/chest circumference, weight), respiratory function, CHOP INTEND, HINE-2 milestones, feeding route, ventilation use — CONFIRMED-VERBATIM (registry outcomes module).
- Sites: ≥6 European countries recorded (Belgium, Bulgaria, Denmark, Germany, Italy, among listed ones); isFdaRegulatedDrug: false; DMC: yes.
- **Program-level status**: registry search `branaplam` (2026-10-19) returns **3** registered studies: NCT02268552 (COMPLETED), NCT05330286 (Phase 1 healthy-adult formulation/food-effect PK comparison, **TERMINATED**), NCT05111249 (TERMINATED). Branaplam is **not approved anywhere** in the checked sources. Whether Novartis formally discontinued the SMA indication is **UNVERIFIABLE from the sources checked** (no registry statement; the SMA trial ran to completion with results posted 2025).
- Pediatric long-term exposure: up to ~7 years in the SMA trial per the VIBRANT-HD paper, with "no risk of axonal degeneration" reported (see qualifier above).
