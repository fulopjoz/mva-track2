# Pharmacological chaperone / corrector literature - Gate-0 evidence memo
> **Addendum 2026-09-08 (wave 2).** `PHARMACOCHAPERONE_LIT2.md` supersedes two
> statements in this memo: rezatapopt is Phase 1/2 (not "registrational"), and
> the Lequeue 2025 validation chain is thinner (no DSF/mammalian) than implied.
> Wave 2 also places the concept falsifier on record (AMP-PNP did not stabilize
> the BubR1 domain in Suijkerbuijk 2012) and upgrades the novelty claim.

Target question: can a small-molecule stabilizer (corrector) plausibly rescue BUB1B p.Asn1002Lys, a partially buried intra-domain packing contact (with residues 977/978) in the BubR1 pseudokinase domain, in mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA)?
Search date: 2026-09-08. Sources: PubMed MCP, RCSB PDB, web search. Every claim carries PMID/DOI. Contact-mapping of N1002 (residues 977/978) is project-internal homology modelling (see `STRUCTURAL_VALIDATION.md`), not external literature.

## 1. Approved chaperone precedents

**Migalastat (Fabry, approved).** Oral pharmacological chaperone (150 mg every other day) that stabilizes amenable mutant α-galactosidase A forms to restore lysosomal trafficking. Phase 3 ATTRACT randomized only **57 adults** vs ERT (18 months, open-label), with 4 patients excluded post hoc as non-amenable by a validated cell-based amenability assay (Hughes et al., 2017, [doi:10.1136/jmedgenet-2016-104178](https://doi.org/10.1136/jmedgenet-2016-104178); PMID 27834756). Durability to 30 months in the open-label extension, n=46 (Feldt-Rasmussen et al., 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.ymgme.2020.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymgme.2020.07.007); PMID 33012654). The **amenable-mutation concept** - an in vitro assay that predicts which missense variants respond - is the field's central regulatory invention and is directly transferable thinking for N1002K. *What it sat on:* Fabry is an X-linked LSD affecting a much larger population than MVA (MVA subtypes are counted in tens of cases; e.g., only 12 CEP57-MVA2 cases in a 2021 review, Santos-Simarro et al., 2021, [doi:10.1016/j.ejmg.2021.104338](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmg.2021.104338), PMID 34500087).

**Ambroxol as chaperone for glucocerebrosidase (repurposed, in trials - not approved as a chaperone).** Identified as a GCase chaperone in Gaucher fibroblasts (Bendikov-Bar et al., 2013, [doi:10.1016/j.bcmd.2012.10.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bcmd.2012.10.007); PMID 23158495). Pilot in neuronopathic Gaucher: n=5, high-dose oral ambroxol + ERT, crossed the blood–brain barrier and improved myoclonus (Narita et al., 2016, [doi:10.1002/acn3.292](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.292); PMID 27042680). Parkinson/GBA1: open-label escalation to **1.26 g/day**, n=17, safe, CSF penetration and CSF GCase protein +35% (Mullin et al., 2020, [doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2019.4611](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2019.4611); PMID 31930374); phase II RCT AMBITIOUS at **1.2 g/day**, 52 weeks (protocol: Colucci et al., 2023, [doi:10.1136/bmjno-2023-000535](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjno-2023-000535); PMID 38027469); **phase 3** ASPro-PD, n=330 planned (protocol: Toffoli et al., 2026, [doi:10.1007/s00415-026-13683-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-026-13683-7); PMID 41708985). Caution: chaperone activity of ambroxol against some GCase variants has been questioned (Cyske et al., 2024, [doi:10.1016/j.neuint.2024.105774](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuint.2024.105774)). *What it sat on:* Gaucher is the most common LSD (Thirumal Kumar et al., 2018, [doi:10.1016/bs.apcsb.2018.10.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.apcsb.2018.10.002); PMID 30635084) and GBA1-PD is a common genetic risk (10–15% of PD; Toffoli et al., 2026) - the phase 3 was built on a 330-patient population unthinkable in MVA.

**CFTR correctors (approved; mechanism class).** Lumacaftor (VX-809) corrects F508del processing, restoring chloride secretion to ~14% of non-CF airway epithelia (EC50 81 nM) (Van Goor et al., 2011, [doi:10.1073/pnas.1105787108](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1105787108); PMID 21976485). Mechanism class: lumacaftor/tezacaftor are type-I correctors binding a hydrophobic pocket in TMD1; elexacaftor is a type-III corrector stabilizing TM10–11 and the TMD/NBD1 interface - genuine pharmacological chaperones (direct binding to the mutant protein), distinguished from proteostasis regulators (Okiyoneda et al., 2024, [doi:10.1007/s00018-024-05295-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-024-05295-z); PMID 38888668). Correctors came from high-throughput screening campaigns of generally low-efficacy hits, needed two-corrector combination + potentiator for clinical impact (Okiyoneda et al., 2024). The peer-reviewed record does **not disclose** the size of Vertex's proprietary screening campaigns (see Gaps). *What it sat on:* >50,000 people with CF in the European registry alone (2019) (Bell et al., 2023, [J Cyst Fibros, sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369222012429](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369222012429)), i.e., ~3 orders of magnitude more patients than MVA.

**Verdict: supports/feasible.** Approved small-molecule correctors exist and the amenable-variant concept is a precedent for N1002K - but every one was developed on rare-disease populations vastly larger than MVA, and single-corrector efficacy alone was historically modest.

## 2. Methodology precedents

- **Integrin precedent: none located.** Searches for "ITGA3 pharmacological chaperone" and integrin chaperone screens (PubMed + web, 2026-09-08) return no published pharmacological-chaperone screen for any integrin; ITGA3 literature is disease-gene discovery (e.g., gain-of-glycosylation ITGA3 mutation causing lung disease/nephrotic syndrome; Nicolaou et al., 2012, PMC3533548, [doi:10.1016/j.ajhg.2012.08.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2012.08.017)). No evidence located for an integrin (or any cell-surface adhesion receptor) chaperone campaign; the proposed integrin angle would be genuinely novel territory, not a methodology precedent.
- **Docking-based computational chaperone studies exist** but are typically small-scale and post-hoc: e.g., docking + MD of known chaperones (ambroxol, NN-DNJ) against N370S/L444P GCase (Thirumal Kumar et al., 2018, [doi:10.1016/bs.apcsb.2018.10.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.apcsb.2018.10.002); PMID 30635084). The closest recent end-to-end template is a **bacterial HTS of 2,320 FDA-approved drugs** for chaperones of missense-destabilized homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase in alkaptonuria, with CB-Dock docking of hits (Lequeue et al., 2025, [doi:10.1016/j.ejphar.2025.178048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2025.178048); PMID 40784658) - note it used CB-Dock, not GNINA.
- **DSF/thermal-shift chaperone screening is established methodology**: a generic protocol for identifying small-molecule folding correctors among metabolites/ligands/cofactors via thermal shift, applied to intracellular metabolic enzymes with folding-defect variants (Lucas, Henriques & Gomes, 2019, [doi:10.1007/978-1-4939-8820-4_16](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4939-8820-4_16); PMID 30341615).
- **GNINA**: GNINA is a deep-learning docking framework (McNutt et al., 2021, [doi:10.1186/s13321-021-00522-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-021-00522-2); v1.3: [doi:10.1186/s13321-025-00973-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-025-00973-x)). No published pharmacological-chaperone discovery screen driven by GNINA was located (PubMed + web, 2026-09-08). A "first GNINA-based pharmacological chaperone screen" claim is therefore *plausibly defensible but only narrowly* - it cannot be a "first computational chaperone screen" (those exist), and a definitive negative cannot be proven from searches; a claim would need wording like "first GNINA-based virtual screen for stabilizers of a mitotic-checkpoint pseudokinase".

**Verdict: open.** Methodology components (DSF, repurposing HTS, docking) all have precedent; the specific GNINA-driven pseudokinase-stabilizer screen appears unoccupied but novelty claims must be narrow and hedged.

## 3. Structural biology of the BubR1 C-terminal kinase domain

- **6JKK** is the crystal structure of the **Drosophila melanogaster** BubR1 kinase domain (apo, 1.85 Å; deposited 2019 by Lin, Ye, Huang, Liu, Zhang, Yao; RCSB [10.2210/pdb6JKK/pdb](https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb6JKK/pdb)). Primary citation: **Huang et al., 2019, *Cell Research* 29:562–578, [doi:10.1038/s41422-019-0178-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-019-0178-z), PMID 31201382** (not Pulido). Key findings: the kinase domain folds into a conformation predicted to be catalytically active; Drosophila BubR1 phosphorylates CENP-E; computational modeling yielded **bubristatin**, a selective antagonist binding the αN1 helix of the N-terminal extension and the αC helix. This is the only isolated experimental kinase-domain structure of any BubR1 ortholog located.
- **Human BUB1B kinase domain: no isolated experimental structure located.** Human BubR1 has been resolved only within cryo-EM assemblies of the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC) and MCC–APC/C; current work exploits those structures (e.g., BubR1 TPR-domain analysis within the resolved MCC–APC/C cryo-EM; Zhang et al., 2026, [doi:10.1038/s42003-026-10384-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10384-9); PMID 42477419). Human-domain modelling therefore rests on the Drosophila template (project-internal, `STRUCTURAL_VALIDATION.md`).
- **Pseudokinase status (human):** human BUBR1 retained the catalytic triad but catalysis is dispensable for error-free chromosome segregation; **ATP-pocket residues are essential for conformational stability** of the domain - an "unusual, triad-containing pseudokinase" (Suijkerbuijk et al., 2012, [doi:10.1016/j.devcel.2012.03.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2012.03.009); PMID 22698286). The pseudokinase domain also scaffolds PP2A–B56 to balance kinase/phosphatase activity at kinetochores (Elowe, 2021, [doi:10.1080/23723556.2021.1876511](https://doi.org/10.1080/23723556.2021.1876511); PMID 33860079). Implication: a nucleotide-pocket-binder could stabilize the fold (as ATP-pocket residues themselves do), but functional rescue must be assayed as checkpoint/scaffolding output, not enzymatic activity.
- **N1002K or nearby variant characterization: none located.** PubMed searches for BUB1B N1002K functional data return nothing variant-specific. The best-characterized MVA BubR1 models are truncating alleles (e.g., 2211insGTTA hypomorphic mouse with progeroid features; Wijshake et al., 2012, [doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1003138](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1003138); PMID 23300461; original five-family BUB1B discovery: Hanks et al., 2004, [doi:10.1038/ng1449](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng1449); PMID 15475955). Destabilization of N1002K is inferred from our modelling plus general buried-contact logic, not from published biophysics.

**Verdict: open.** The template structure exists at high quality but is Drosophila; the human domain's stability determinants (ATP-pocket dependence) are documented and favorable to a stabilization concept; no direct experimental handle on N1002K has been published.

## 4. What would falsify the corrector concept

- **ΔΔG thresholds.** There is no universal clinical cutoff, but practice calls a variant "destabilized" at roughly **>1–2 kcal/mol** predicted ΔΔG. Grounding: the distribution of stability effects of all possible point mutations in globular single-domain proteins is bi-Gaussian with means ~0.6 kcal/mol (surface) and ~1.4 kcal/mol (core) (Tokuriki et al., 2007, [doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2007.03.069](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2007.03.069); PMID 17482644); destabilization is the dominant deleterious effect of mutations and a major constraint on evolvability (Tokuriki & Tawfik, 2009, [doi:10.1016/j.sbi.2009.08.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbi.2009.08.003); PMID 19765975). Falsification trigger: if N1002K's measured/predicted ΔΔG is near the core-substitution mean (~1–1.5 kcal/mol) after accounting for method error (same order of magnitude as the effect), the "destabilized variant, chaperone-rescuable" premise weakens; a buried Asn→Lys substitution losing packing contacts with 977/978 plausibly exceeds the core mean, but this must be shown experimentally (DSF thermal shift of the purified domain, wild-type vs mutant).
- **Intracellular globular target vs membrane/secretory targets.** Approved chaperones (migalastat; ambroxol if approved) act on **soluble lysosomal enzymes whose rescue exploits ER quality control and trafficking** - a bottleneck that does not exist for a cytosolic mitotic checkpoint protein degraded by the cytosolic UPS. A cytoplasmic pseudokinase is therefore an outlier among *approved* chaperone targets. However, the concept is not unprecedented for intracellular globular domains: **rezatapopt (PC14586)** binds a pocket created by the thermally destabilizing p53 Y220C mutation, stabilizing the intranuclear DNA-binding domain and restoring function - now in a registrational phase 2 trial (Vu et al., 2024, [doi:10.1021/acsmedchemlett.4c00379](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsmedchemlett.4c00379); PMID 39811143; Puzio-Kuter et al., 2025, [doi:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-24-1421](https://doi.org/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-24-1421); PMID 39945593); chaperone approaches to intracellular cancer proteins (p53, BRAF, NQO1) are reviewed in Betancor-Fernández et al., 2018, [doi:10.1007/164_2017_55](https://doi.org/10.1007/164_2017_55); PMID 28993836.
- **Published failure modes of chaperone campaigns.** (i) *Amenability ceilings*: migalastat excludes non-amenable GLA variants defined by a cell-based assay (Hughes et al., 2017) - the N1002K equivalent must pass a cell-based rescue gate before any in-human extrapolation. (ii) *Variant-specific chaperone failures*: ambroxol's chaperone action is contested for some GCase variants (Cyske et al., 2024). (iii) *Modest single-corrector efficacy*: first-generation CFTR correctors alone gave only ~14% of wild-type function and required combination therapy (Van Goor et al., 2011; Okiyoneda et al., 2024). (iv) *Pseudokinase-specific risk*: because human BubR1 catalysis is dispensable (Suijkerbuijk et al., 2012), an ATP-pocket stabilizer must not disrupt the domain's scaffolding interactions (PP2A–B56 anchoring; Elowe, 2021) - a gain-of-stability that perturbs the MCC assembly surface would be falsifying.
- **Structural falsifier.** Human numbering of the N1002 contact pair (977/978) rests on homology to a Drosophila apo structure; if the human-domain model (or a future human structure) shows the mutated site solvent-exposed or in a flexible loop with ΔΔG ≈ 0, the destabilization premise fails.

**Verdict: open - falsifiable at defined gates** (ΔΔG magnitude + mutant DSF thermal shift; cell-based checkpoint rescue assay for N1002K-BubR1; no perturbation of scaffolding interfaces). The intracellular-cytosolic location is an outlier vs approved chaperones but not a falsifier given the p53-Y220C precedent.

## Gaps (what literature could NOT settle)

1. **Vertex's corrector screening campaign size** (compounds screened for VX-809/VX-445) is not disclosed in the peer-reviewed literature - only accounts of "high-throughput screening" exist (Okiyoneda et al., 2024); "R&D scale" claims must stay qualitative.
2. **No GNINA-based pharmacological chaperone screen was located** - a negative result from PubMed/web searches, not proof of absence; novelty wording must be narrow.
3. **No human BUB1B kinase-domain experimental structure** (isolated); all human-domain structural claims rest on Drosophila 6JKK homology and cryo-EM of larger assemblies.
4. **No variant-specific functional/biophysical data for BUB1B N1002K** (or residues 977/978) anywhere located; the destabilization claim currently rests on internal modelling.
5. **No integrin pharmacological-chaperone precedent** exists, so the "ITGA3 analogy" cannot be sourced - it can only be stated as an open field.
6. **MVA total/BUB1B patient counts are not firmly settled in the literature** (Hanks 2004: five families; CEP57 subtype: 12 cases as of 2021; no registry-backed BUB1B-MVA census located).
7. **Sapropterin/PAH** as an approved cytosolic-enzyme chaperone precedent was not verified during this sweep - would need a dedicated pass if the intracellular-target argument leans on it.
