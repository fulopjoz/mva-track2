# Wave 3 - Q8 ImmunoSurveillanceNK: is "enhance endogenous clearance of aneuploid cells" a real pediatric-repurposing axis?

pulled per load-bearing paper from scite.ai. Grades: **A** = human RCT ·
**B** = human clinical/observational · **C** = human cells / independent-lab animal · **D** = mouse-only /

**Termination criterion (declared before searching):** each sub-question carries ≥2 verified primary

---

## VERDICT (one paragraph)

**"Enhance endogenous immune clearance of aneuploid cells" is a REAL, mechanistically-established biology
in untransformed human cells - and a FANTASY as a pediatric intervention today.** The phenomenon is not
in dispute: human cell-culture and in-vivo mouse data show that acutely mis-segregating / newly aneuploid
untransformed cells arrest, senesce, and are eliminated by natural killer (NK) cells through NF-κB-driven
pro-inflammatory signaling (Santaguida 2017; Wang 2021; Guern/Hernandez 2016 - the latter via NKG2D/DNAM-1
ligand upregulation). It is even documented *in a human constitutional-CIN patient*: the 2022 MAD1L1-MVA
report shows systemic inflammation, enhanced interferon/NF-κB, and γδ-T-cell clonal expansion in a germline
SAC-defective individual (Villarroya-Beltri 2022) - i.e., the body *does* run an immune reaction to its own
aneuploidy. **But there is no pediatric-feasible lever to steer it.** The pediatric pharmacopoeia contains
no chronic, safe, whole-child NK-enhancing intervention: the only mature NK-boosting cytokines (IL-2,
IL-15) have no chronic-pediatric profile suitable for a healthy 5-year-old (low-dose IL-2 is Treg-driving
and used only as GVHD therapy; systemic IL-15 pediatric administration = no evidence located); checkpoint
agents are oncology-only and excluded for age 5; and the *one* chronic-pediatric immunomodulator on the
candidate table (everolimus) points the **wrong way** - mTORC1 inhibition suppresses NK development and
cytolytic function (Marçais 2014, incl. humans) and reduces peripheral NK number/maturation in human
cancer patients (Besson 2021, J Immunol). **Net: the axis should not be added as a candidate.** What
it *does* contribute is (i) a **safety flag** on the existing everolimus candidacy (NK-count monitoring)
and (ii) a **mechanistic justification for strict surveillance-only default** in MVA (the host's own
clearance is the tumor-suppressor; do not blunt it). The tension with everolimus is **real but
quantitatively unmeasured in children** - it does not disqualify everolimus (whose rationale is tissue
proteostasis rescue, not NK), but it must be operationalized or the rapalog arm and the host's clearance
mechanism are in direct conflict.

## 2. Pediatric-feasible immunomodulation census (EMPTY for the preventive direction)

If the axis were actionable, we'd need a chronic, safe, whole-child intervention that **raises** NK
surveillance without oncology-grade toxicity. Census result: **the preventive direction is EMPTY.** The
only human pediatric NK-relevant agents are oncology/transplant-setting and unsuitable for prophylaxis in
a healthy 5-year-old.

### 2a. mTOR inhibitors (the tension - see §5)
Chronic-pediatric-safe (EXIST-1/3; cross-ref wave2_CINSyndromeAxis), but the **direction is anti-NK**:
mTORC1 is essential for NK development/cytolytic function (Marçais 2014, mice + humans; s65/c2),
and everolimus in human cancer patients contracts the peripheral NK pool and lowers maturation
(J Immunol 2021; s0/c0/m4). **Not an NK-enhancer.**

### 2b. IL-2
- **High-dose / ADCC-combination pediatric oncology:** dinutuximab + IL-2 in high-risk neuroblastoma
  (*Ladenstein 2018, Lancet Oncol*, [10.1016/s1470-2045(18)30578-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1470-2045(18)30578-3), PMID 30442501; the *Yu 2010, NEJM* ANBL0032 backbone, [10.1056/NEJMoa0911123](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911123), PMID 20879881). Real pediatric IL-2 exposure, but **active-disease immunotherapy with substantial toxicity (capillary-leak syndrome, etc.)** - not a prevention agent.
- **Low-dose IL-2, pediatric cGVHD:** *Whangbo 2019, Blood Adv* ([10.1182/bloodadvances.2019000631](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2019000631), PMID 31471324) and *2023 Blood Adv* real-world children/young adults ([10.1182/bloodadvances.2023009729](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2023009729), PMID 37603347). **But low-dose IL-2 is a Treg- (and NK-) expansion therapy used to *treat chronic immunosuppression-dominated GVHD* - the opposite of tumor surveillance.** Not a preventive lever.
- **Verdict:** IL-2 is either too toxic (high-dose) or wrong-direction (low-dose Treg). **Not candidate.**

### 2c. IL-15
- Systemic IL-15 in children: **no evidence located** (PubMed query `"IL-15" AND pediatric AND (trial OR administration)` → only ex-vivo/adoptive/one ex-vivo stimulated NK-cell therapy in pediatric leukemia, *Am J Hematol 2025*; and an *in-vitro* IL-2+IL-15 NKG2D induction against Wilms tumor, *J Oncol 2022* - laboratory/ex-vivo only). No chronic pediatric IL-15 agonist (e.g. NKTR-255) prophylaxis data.
- **Verdict:** no systemic pediatric IL-15 exists. **Impossible today.**

### 2d. NK-cell adoptive therapy
- *Rubnitz 2010, JCO* NKAML haploidentical NK infusions in childhood AML ([10.1200/JCO.2009.24.4590](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2009.24.4590), PMID 20085940). True pediatric human NK data, but **cell therapy in active leukemia**, not a prophylactic leverage for a healthy child. Out of scope.

### 2e. Checkpoint-adjacent
- Excluded per assignment for age 5 (pediatric checkpoint use is oncology-only, e.g. TMB-H tumors). **Not a prevention route.** Not censused further.

### 2f. Everything else (negative)
- No chronic pediatric-safe small-molecule "NK-booster" with human pediatric data was located (glycans,
  lenalidomide-class, AhR modulators, HDACi re NKG2D = no chronic pediatric prophylactic profile).
  **Recorded negative, not an omission.**

## 3. Does innate immune exhaustion/editing of mosaic aneuploid cells appear in constitutional-CIN literature?

**YES - and it is the single most useful by-product of this census.**

- **Villarroya-Beltri 2022 (Sci Adv, MAD1L1-MVA):** a constitutional-CIN human patient shows *systemic
  inflammation + interferon/NF-κB in aneuploid and euploid cells alike + clonal γδ-T expansion* - i.e.,
  direct in-human evidence of an immune reaction to lifelong mosaic aneuploidy. Grade B/C.
- **Malumbres & Villarroya-Beltri 2024, *Nat Rev Genet* [10.1038/s41576-024-00762-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-024-00762-6) · PMID 39169218:** MVA review explicitly lists "cellular and systemic stress, including **chronic immune response** and the associated clinical implications" as a feature of MVA (abstract). Grade B (review; not OA, so details read at abstract level - flagged).
- **Mad2/MAD1L1 and JCI 2020:** the wave-1 *JCI 2020* BubR1 allelic-series paper does **not** report immune phenotyping (abstract checked - no immun/inflamm/interferon mention); so the immune-editing observation is currently **MAD1L1-specific**, not yet documented for BUB1B/CEP57/TRIP13. **Gap** for the proband genotype.
- **Countervailing evolution logic:** in *tumors*, high aneuploidy/CIN selection drives **immune escape**, not clearance - Davies/Taylor: Davoli 2017 (*Science*, [10.1126/science.aaf8399](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf8399), PMID 28104840) shows highly aneuploid human tumors are immune-cold (reduced CD8 infiltrate, worse immunotherapy response; s129/c16); *Wang 2021* itself shows NF-κB-driven clearance is overcome in cancer cells; **Plk1-driven chronic CIN in a Her2+ breast model** upregulates PD-L1/CD206 and drives immune suppression (Cell Rep 2023, [10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113266](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113266), PMID 37979172). So the *same* aneuploidy that is NK-cleared when nascent becomes immune-suppressive once a clone escapes. This is the inescapable dual-edge that makes *intervention* dangerous without precise staging.

**Interpretation for MVA:** the host's endogenous aneuploid clearance likely acts as a tumor-suppressive
brake on emerging escape clones (consistent with wave-2 F6 objection to senolysis). "Immune-editing of
mosaic aneuploid cells" is therefore a **real phenomenon in a human CIN syndrome** - but it is the
*default surveillance state*, not a drug target.

## 4. The everolimus-candidacy vs NK-surveillance tension: RESOLVED as a real, flag-and-monitor conflict

**Question posed:** does chronic mTORi suppress the very clearance mechanism the host relies on?

**Answer - YES, mechanistically grounded, and it is unresolved in children (quantitatively).**

Evidence that chronic mTORC1 inhibition suppresses NK (the clearance mechanism):

1. **Marçais et al. 2014, *Nat Immunol* [10.1038/ni.2936](https://doi.org/10.1038/ni.2936) · PMID 24973821**
   (s65/c2/m723): IL-15→mTORC1 is *essential* for NK proliferation during development and for cytolytic
   potential during inflammation/viral infection; "**rapamycin inhibited NK-cell cytotoxicity both in mice
   and humans; this probably contributes to the immunosuppressive activity of this drug in different
   clinical settings.**"
2. **Human everolimus data, *J Immunol* 2021 [10.4049/jimmunol.2001215](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.2001215) · PMID 33931486**
   (s0/c0/m4): in metastatic-breast-cancer patients on everolimus 3 mo, **contraction of the peripheral NK
   pool and a decrease in NK maturation** (mTORC1 inhibited, mTORC2 preserved); "first in-vivo
   characterization of the role of mTOR in human NK cells." Adult oncology setting.
3. Corroborating mechanism: rapamycin (but not CsA/FK506) alters NK function (*Transplantation* 2008,
   [10.1097/01.tp.0000296817.28053.7b](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.tp.0000296817.28053.7b), PMID 18192925);
   sirolimus affects NK reconstitution after allo-HSCT (*Transplant Cell Ther* 2023,
   [10.1016/j.jtct.2023.03.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtct.2023.03.023), PMID 36966873); TGF-β represses
   NK via mTOR (*Sci Signal* 2016, [10.1126/scisignal.aad1884](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.aad1884), PMID 26884601).

Dose-region counterpoint (why it is not a blanket "no"):

4. **Mannick et al. 2018, *Sci Transl Med* [10.1126/scitranslmed.aaq1564](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aaq1564) ·
   PMID 29997249** (s8/c0/m396): *low-dose* TORC1 inhibition (RTB101 ± low-dose everolimus) **enhanced**
   immune function and reduced infections in elderly adults - i.e., there is a low-dose
   immunoenhancing/anti-inflammatory window in adults (not NK-specific; monocyte/macrophage + PD-L1 axis).
5. Mouse model: everolimus can *enhance* TRAIL-mediated antitumor activity of liver-resident NK cells
   (*Transpl Int* 2020, [10.1111/tri.13536](https://doi.org/10.1111/tri.13536), PMID 31560810) - a direct
   counter-direction datum, **mouse only**.

**Resolution (not a full contradiction, but a load-bearing safety flag):**

- The **everolimus candidacy's rationale is tissue proteostasis rescue** in mosaic tissues (raising
  autophagic/proteostatic capacity; wave-2 Level-3), **not** NK-mediated clearance - so a direct
  mechanistic *contradiction* of the candidacy is not asserted. The candidacy stands on its own (weak,
  Grade-D) footing.
- **However,** the chronic-mTORi arm and the host's aneuploid-clearance mechanism are in **genuine
  tension**: if everolimus both rescues mosaic tissue proteostasis AND suppresses the NK that remove
  emerging aneuploid escape clones, the net cancer-risk effect is mathematically indeterminate and
  **directionally unmeasurable today**.
- **Operational resolution (concrete):** any chronic-rapalog arm **must** carry (i) NK count/maturation
  monitoring (peripheral CD56ᴰⁱᵐ/ᴮʳⁱᵍʰᵗ + NKG2D/DNAM-1 receptor readout), (ii) a dose-hold criterion on NK
  suppression and on any γδ/NF-κB-inflammation signal, and (iii) intensified tumor surveillance  - 
  mirroring the wave-2 F6 gate vocabulary. This resolution is **adopted as recommended practice**, not
  verified by any controlled pediatric study (none located).
- **Pediatric-specific gap (explicit):** no pediatric (age <18) data on NK numbers/function under chronic
  everolimus/sirolimus for TSC were located in this census (TSC EXIST trials report infections but not NK
  phenotyping). **"No evidence located"** - would be settled by an NK immunophenotyping add-on to any
  existing TSC-everolimus cohort.

## 5. Verdict & recommendations

1. **Verdict:** the axis as a *drug-candidate* ("enhance aneuploid-cell NK clearance") is **not viable
   in a 5-year-old today** - pediatric-feasible lever missing (EMPTY census) and the one feasible
   chronic immunomodulator (rapalog) points the wrong way.
2. **Do NOT add any new NK-enhancement candidate** to candidates.tsv.
3. **Fold the axis into the existing everolimus candidacy as a mandatory monitoring flag** (NK counts,
   γδ/NF-κB inflammation, dose-hold + intensified surveillance) - this is the actionable takeaway.
4. **Reinforce the surveillance-only default:** the endogenous aneuploid-clearance brake is a
   tumor-suppressor; from this axis it is a fresh, human-syndrome-grounded reason to avoid
   broadly immunosuppressive or anti-inflammatory chronic intervention in MVA (also reinforces wave-2 F6
   against senolysis).
5. **Feasible research enrichment (cheap, within consent envelope):** add an **immune/NF-κB panel**
   (plasma IFN/NF-κB-cytokine signature; PBMC NK/γδ immunophenotype) to Gate-0 patient-cell work - this
   converts the MAD1L1 observation into a BUB1B-proband question and would empirically resolve the
   everolimus-NK tension in the actual genotype.
6. **Note for the report frame:** the NK-clearance mechanism (Santaguida/Wang) and the MAD1L1-MVA
   inflammation are real biology; they should be quoted as evidence that the host already surveils its
   aneuploid cells - never as a claim that a drug can enhance it in a child.

## Gaps / explicit limitations

- **"Xu 2024" NK-receptor–ligand-on-CIN-lines paper: no evidence located** (searched PubMed multiple
  query shapes, web; scite partner-search endpoint unauthorized in this session).
- **No pediatric NK data under chronic rapalog** (TSC cohort phenotyping absent) - the tension
  resolution rests on adult-oncology (J Immunol 2021) + mechanistic (Marçais 2014) extrapolation.
- **MAD1L1 immune findings are one patient/syndrome-family**, not yet replicated or extended to
  BUB1B/CEP57/TRIP13 MVA; Malumbres review read at abstract level (not OA).
- **Interventions census is by definition absence-evidence** (search date 2026-09-05): recorded
  negatives are probabilities, not proofs of impossibility.
- scite contradiction tallies noted where large (Santaguida 2017 c5; Marçais c2; Davoli c16) - none
  entail retraction/editorial notice; no notices on any load-bearing paper (checked via scite).
